The pinned reproduction of the artifact recorded in
`openspec/style/blog-voice-calibration.md`: `&sect;` ends in a semicolon, and
an extractor that counts before it decodes reads every statute citation as
punctuation the author chose. Twelve citations in one predecessor post
inflated its measured semicolon rate from a real 3.28 per 1,000 words to a
reported 11.1, and both previously published semicolon maxima were this
artifact.

The bill amends &sect;22757.1, &sect;22757.2 and &sect;22757.3, adds
&sect;22757.4 and &sect;22757.5, and leaves &sect;22757.6 alone. The
enforcement sections are &sect;22757.7 and &sect;22757.8. The definitions sit
at &sect;22757.9, the exemptions at &sect;22757.10, the effective date at
&sect;22757.11, and the sunset clause at &sect;22757.12.

The body carries no semicolon its author typed. Decoded, the rate is zero.
Undecoded, it is twelve marks over a short piece, which clears the 2.5 line
several times over.
