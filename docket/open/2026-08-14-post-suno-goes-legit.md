## Why now

Suno, the music-generation tool already in this site's Directory, spent the last week announcing a coordinated turn toward industry legitimacy, all at its own blog (retrieved this run) and covered by The Verge and Ars Technica:

- **Studio 2.0 (13 August 2026)** — a browser-based DAW ("the browser-based DAW that lets you move at the speed of your ideas") that adds MIDI support and custom effects, moving Suno from prompt-to-song toward a real music-production tool (The Verge's take, retrieved this run).
- **BMG global alliance (12 August 2026)** — a "landmark global partnership" that settles prior use of BMG recordings and publishing works, compensates participating artists/songwriters, and precedes Suno's first music model "developed in partnership with the music industry" (Suno's announcement; The Verge notes the BMG deal).
- **Downloads policy and Terms changes (announced 10 August, effective 3 September 2026)** — download limits and updated Terms, which Ars Technica (6 August, retrieved this run) reports Suno frames as anti-abuse: watermarks on AI-generated music and limits on downloading tracks to stop "large-scale abuse", positioned as how Suno "hopes to go legit".

Why this site: Suno is a Directory entry; the entry's description ("...") and verified date date from before this cluster, so the Directory may warrant a description/verification update as well as the post. The story an AI enthusiast would send to someone: the most-used AI music tool is voluntarily adopting watermarks, a publishing settlement, and a DAW — the opposite direction from the same-week news about Amazon training on Twitch by default (filed separately this round). It is current, checkable, and assembled from Suno's own blog plus two tech-press sources retrieved this run.

The post must not overstate: the BMG alliance is a partnership announcement, not proof of any cleared legal status; the watermark and download-limit plans are Suno's stated intentions (Ars's framing), with the Terms change effective 3 September; Studio 2.0's capabilities should be described as Suno presents them, and any "first" claims attributed.
