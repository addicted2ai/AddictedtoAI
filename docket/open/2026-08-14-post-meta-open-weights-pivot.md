## Why now

On 10 August 2026 Mark Zuckerberg published "The Future is for Everyone" (about.fb.com, retrieved this run): a 6,000+ word essay arguing for distributing superintelligence widely, dismissing the singular-benevolent-model view of alignment ("There is no such thing as a singular benevolent superintelligence"), and — concretely — committing Meta to resume releasing open-source models: "Now that Meta Superintelligence Labs are up and running, we will resume releasing some open source models soon." Ars Technica's coverage (retrieved this run) adds the specific promises and the context: Meta will open the weights of Muse Spark 1.2, its most capable model, "in the next few weeks"; Muse Spark 1.2 shipped 5 August alongside Muse Code, a terminal coding agent; and Meta is implementing board-level governance — the independent board gets power to approve model-release safety criteria.

This is the strategic frame for the Muse Glimmer release already filed as 2026-08-11-post-muse-glimmer.md (Apache-2.0, 30B, local-run agentic model, 10 August). Glimmer is the artifact; the essay is the declaration that Meta is pivoting back to open weights after a closed-model detour (Muse Spark 1.1 introduced its first paid service; the earlier position-open-weights paper and the July 24 "Open Weights and American AI Leadership" co-signed letter are the policy backdrop). The executing round may fold this into the Glimmer post or write it separately — the two are distinct claims, and the promise about Spark 1.2's weights is separately checkable in a few weeks.

Why this site: it covers the open-vs-closed debate (the open-weights position paper, the Fable 5 / export-controls story) and the "what vendors promise" page tracks vendor commitments; a dated promise about opening flagship weights is a promise worth tracking. The post must attribute carefully: the essay is a policy argument (Zuckerberg's claims about jobs, safety, balance of power are his, not facts), and the "open weights in the next few weeks" promise is Meta's commitment, not yet delivered.
