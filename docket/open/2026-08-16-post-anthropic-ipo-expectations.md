## Why now

On 13 August 2026 the Financial Times (syndicated by Ars Technica, fetched this run) reported that half a dozen Anthropic backers expect the company to float at a valuation of $2 trillion or more in October, which would eclipse SpaceX's June debut and make Anthropic's the largest-ever initial public offering. This is the ownership-and-business side of the price-war and export-control stories the site already covers, and it is dated, attributed (to unnamed backers), and checkable against figures Anthropic itself has published (the May $47B annualized-revenue announcement).

The facts, as the FT piece states them:

- **The expectation.** Backers expect a float at "$2 trillion or more" in October; a listing at that level "would eclipse SpaceX and make the AI lab's debut the largest-ever initial public offering." (SpaceX went public at a $1.77 trillion valuation in June, per the same article.)
- **The revenue case.** Backers expect Anthropic's annualized revenue to be between $100 billion and $120 billion by end of 2026, using the startup's preferred measure (full-year sales inferred from recent performance) — "up by more than 10 times over the course of 2026." Anthropic announced in May that annualized revenue had surpassed $47 billion. One investor's model: "If Anthropic is growing 800 percent a year, you'd think at the incredibly low end they would trade at 30 times [revenue]. That would make them a $3 trillion company." Anthropic has no publicly listed US peer; AI-beneficiary comparables Palantir and Nebius have traded at roughly 55× revenue this year.
- **What is not fixed.** Senior executives had yet to fix the valuation target "even in private conversations," per several investors. Anthropic declined to comment; it is in an SEC quiet period after filing paperwork in June.
- **The risks the article names.** Chinese competition, AI regulation pressure, a "simmering feud with the US government" — including the Commerce Department's temporary ban on its best models (June export controls forced Anthropic to briefly pull Fable 5 and Mythos 5), which two investors say contributed to June's revenue-growth slowdown. Ramp data shows Anthropic's market share among US businesses rose last month, but "businesses were hitting their limit on AI spend" and turning to cheaper alternatives; Artificial Analysis finds Anthropic's market-leading model costs more than 2.5× OpenAI's flagship to use, while Chinese open-weight alternatives are a fraction of the cost.
- **Fundraising context.** VCs, sovereign wealth funds and other institutions have poured just under $100 billion into Anthropic in 2026; its valuation reached $965 billion in May, passing OpenAI's for the first time.

Why this site: the site already carries the export-control story (the Fable 5 post), the price war (open item 2026-08-14-post-openai-anthropic-price-war), and the OpenAI-side valuation/enterprise story. The IPO expectation is the missing piece of the same picture: whether a $2T float is plausible, and which claims about Anthropic's revenue are the company's own versus backers'. The post must attribute the numbers as FT-sourced backer expectations, keep the "$100–120B" figure tied to Anthropic's own preferred-measure caveat, and not treat the $2T valuation as decided — the article says executives had not fixed it.
