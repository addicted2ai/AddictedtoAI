## Why now

On 14 August 2026 Reuters reported (via The Verge, fetched this run) that Apple has trained a custom large language model for the China market in partnership with Alibaba, with the Chinese company's support, citing three unnamed people familiar with the matter. It would also make Apple the first foreign company approved by Beijing to offer a proprietary AI model in China, Reuters said. This is a market-structure story — who may run an AI model in China and how — that the site's existing China coverage (the state-of-open-models item, the price-war item) does not touch, and it is dated and checkable against the regulatory record the article names.

The facts, as The Verge/Reuters state them:

- **The report.** Apple developed the China-focused LLM in partnership with Alibaba and trained it "with the company's support," per Reuters, citing three unnamed people familiar with the matter.
- **Why it matters.** Developing a custom model is a departure from Apple's previous strategy in China: historically Apple used domestic Chinese models to bring generative AI to its devices sold there, because the US models it uses elsewhere (like OpenAI's ChatGPT) are not available.
- **The rollout context.** Apple is gearing up to release Apple Intelligence in China — sources told Reuters it should happen "in the coming months after an update to its iOS operating system." Apple officially registered the on-device generative AI service with China's cyberspace regulator last month (July), clearing a major regulatory hurdle. AI models must be registered with and cleared by the Chinese government before public release.
- **The claim to be careful with.** Reuters says the arrangement "would also make Apple the first foreign company approved by Beijing to offer a proprietary AI model in China" — an expectation, not a completed approval. The "first US company" phrasing is The Verge's paraphrase, not Reuters'; attribute Reuters' own wording to Reuters and The Verge's wording to The Verge.
- **Apple's response.** Apple did not immediately respond to The Verge's request for comment.

Why this site: the site tracks model availability, pricing and the China-vs-US open-model picture (state-of-open-models item; price-war item; Fable 5 export-controls post). This is the consumer-device side of the same divide: what model runs on an iPhone sold in China is decided by Beijing's registration regime, and Apple — which had been sourcing domestic models — is switching to one it trained itself. The post must attribute the reporting to Reuters via The Verge's three unnamed sources, keep the first-company claim as an expectation with Reuters' own wording ("first foreign company approved by Beijing"), and not state the registration or approval as completed facts beyond what the article says (Apple registered the service last month; the model's own approval status is not reported).
