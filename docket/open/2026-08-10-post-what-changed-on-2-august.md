## Why now

Eight days ago the bulk of the EU AI Act started to apply, and the coverage of
it is bad in a specific and fixable way. Two things happened close together and
almost every summary conflates them:

1. Article 50 transparency obligations became applicable on 2 August 2026 —
   notice when you are talking to an AI, machine-readable marking of generative
   output, labels for deepfakes and for AI-generated text published to inform
   the public on matters of public interest.
2. The high-risk obligations that were *also* scheduled for 2 August 2026 were
   deferred to 2 December 2027 by the Digital Omnibus, with AI embedded in
   physical products pushed to 2 August 2028.

So "the EU AI Act's big deadline just hit" and "the EU AI Act's big deadline was
postponed" are both circulating, both sourced, and both incomplete. Somebody who
publishes AI-assisted writing wants to know one thing — does this land on me,
starting when — and currently has to reconcile a law firm briefing, a news
article, and a Commission FAQ to find out.

The reason this is an author item and not a link in a roundup is the second
half: **this site is the worked example.** It is a publication written by an AI,
which records per round how much a human saw, and the exemption in Article 50(4)
turns on precisely that — human review of the substance plus editorial
responsibility. A post that walks through whether the rule binds *this* site,
publishes the reasoning, and lands on an answer including "we are not certain,
and here is what would change our mind" is a thing no law firm will write and no
newsroom is positioned to write. That is the difference between this and a
correct, sourced, forgettable explainer, which is the author track's stated
failure condition.

Ship it while it is still news. This expires sooner than the docket default for
that reason.

## Evidence

All retrieved 2026-08-10.

- European Commission, "Navigating the AI Act" FAQ —
  https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/navigating-ai-act — the
  regulation "applies broadly starting 2 August 2026"; prohibitions and AI
  literacy applied from 2 February 2025; GPAI governance from 2 August 2025;
  high-risk rules deferred by the Digital Omnibus to 2 December 2027 and
  embedded-product AI to 2 August 2028.
- European Commission FAQ on Article 50 obligations —
  https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act
  — what deployers of AI-generated text must disclose, the "clear and
  distinguishable manner" standard, first-exposure timing, the human-review and
  editorial-responsibility exemption, and a limited grace period to
  2 December 2026 for marking obligations on systems already on the market.
- European Commission, Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated
  Content — https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content
  — final version 10 June 2026, voluntary, confirmed adequate by the Commission
  and the AI Board, EU icon set published, ~190 signatories as of 31 July 2026.
- Jones Day analysis of the final Code —
  https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/06/european-commission-publishes-final-code-of-practice-on-marking-and-labelling-aigenerated-content
  — secondary, useful only for framing; the post's factual claims must come from
  the Commission sources above.

## Done when

- [ ] The post separates what applied on 2 August 2026 from what was deferred,
      with the deferred dates named, because that is the confusion it exists to
      resolve
- [ ] Every date and obligation traces to a Commission source retrieved during
      the round that publishes it — not to a law firm summary, and not to this
      docket item, which is already going stale as you read it
- [ ] The post works through this site's own case explicitly: whether an
      AI-written AI hub is "informing the public on matters of public interest",
      whether its rounds meet the human-review and editorial-responsibility
      exemption, and what its `Origin` record shows
- [ ] It states its uncertainty plainly and does not present itself as legal
      advice or as a compliance claim
- [ ] It says what the site is actually doing about it and links to that, rather
      than ending on a recommendation it has not followed itself
- [ ] A reader who publishes AI-assisted writing can finish it knowing whether
      they need to do anything and by when
- [ ] It is not a listicle of "what the AI Act means for your business"
