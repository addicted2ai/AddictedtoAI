## Why now

On 11 August 2026 Manus announced (blog post, fetched this run) that it will "return to operating as an independent company," after Meta acquired the agentic-AI startup for $2 billion in December 2025 — an acquisition China blocked. The announcement is user-actionable: data generated by some users on/after 29 December 2025 (the acquisition date Manus states) will be deleted on 23–24 August 2026, with a backup window open until 7:59 a.m. SGT on 23 August. This is a concrete, dated, checkable story about the agent-tool space the site's Directory covers, with a deadline a reader could act on.

The facts, from Manus's own post and The Verge's coverage:

- **The split.** "Manus will soon return to operating as an independent company." The post's FAQ: "This is part of our separation from Meta; we must take this step to comply with regulatory requirements in specific parts of the world."
- **The data deletion.** Data generated by certain users on/after 29 December 2025 (when Meta acquired Manus, per the FAQ) will be deleted from 8:00 a.m. 23 August through 24 August 2026 (SGT). Affected users can back up from now until 7:59 a.m. SGT 23 August and restore starting 8:00 a.m. SGT 25 August. Affected users lose access for the two-day window; unaffected users take no action. Manus says it is not charging affected users during the backup period.
- **Not a breach.** The FAQ states explicitly: "No. This measure stems from Manus transitioning to independent operations and to comply with regulatory requirements. It is not the result of any security incident."
- **The backstory, per The Verge.** Meta acquired Manus for $2 billion last year and added some of its tools to Meta platforms; China blocked the deal; Manus now returns to independent operation. (The Verge piece is short and links the acquisition and the block as its own prior coverage.)

Why this site: Manus is a general-purpose AI agent — the Directory's Agents category covers exactly this product class — and the story is the market-and-regulation side of the agent coverage the site already has (agent products, agent behavior, the Meta open-weights item). The post's checkable core is the backup deadline and the FAQ's explicit "not a data breach" answer; the executing round must fetch the Manus post itself and keep the $2B figure and the China-block framing attributed to reporting, not to Manus.
