## Finding 2 (blocking) — third failure of the same shape: `.log-lead`'s pages

`app/globals.css:182-183` states `.log-lead` appears "on three pages with no
`<article>` wrapper at all (/log, /log/early, /log/archive)". Repeated at
`CHANGELOG.md` ("the entire freeform prose of `/log`, `/log/early` and
`/log/archive`") and in the docket item's correction 1.

It is a fourth route, not three pages. `app/log/rounds/[id]/page.js:43`
renders `<p className="log-lead">` inside a top-level `<div>` (line 38). No
`<article>` in the file; `app/layout.js` is the only layout and contains no
`<article>`. That route is statically generated by `generateStaticParams()`
over `getPagedLog()` with `dynamicParams = false`; I evaluated it: **101
pages**, every one listed in `app/sitemap.js:148`. So the true figure is 104
pages across four route families, and on each of the 101 `.log-lead` is the
page's only freeform prose paragraph — exactly the role the passage says it
plays on the other three.

The evidence was already in hand. `grep -rln "log-lead" app` returns six
files; the text accounts for five. The cited command, `grep -rn "<article"
app/log/`, is recursive over `app/log/` — its own scope already covers
`app/log/rounds/[id]/page.js`, and its "no matches" result is true of that
file too.

This is the same defect as the first two: a claim about where a class renders,
published as a complete enumeration, without checking every file it renders
in — in the one paragraph in this repository whose stated subject is the harm
of publishing a truncated enumeration as complete. The conclusion survives
(it strengthens the "fits neither story" point), which is why this is a
correction rather than a reversal.
