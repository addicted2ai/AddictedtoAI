## 2. `description` and `excerpt` — the field/world distinction holds

This is the one the coordinator flagged as blocking if it collapsed a third
time. It does not collapse, in either string.

**`description`:** "…**the page's own metadata records** an edit at 3:09 a.m.
on 18 August…". Subject *the page's own metadata*, verb *records*; the clock
time and date are the content of the record, not an assertion about the world.
The truth-conditions are about the field: if the stamp were wrong about the
absolute instant, the sentence would still be true, because the metadata would
still record that. That is the test, and it passes.

**`excerpt`:** "**The page's own metadata dates that edit to** 18 August, the
day account updates began…". Even cleaner — "X dates Y to Z" is unambiguously a
statement about what X assigns. It carries no clock time at all.

One honest observation rather than a defect: the excerpt's appositive gloss
"the day account updates began" is the closest either string comes to the
inference the title was rewritten to avoid, and a reader could draw it. It
survives because the sentence's subject and verb keep the claim about the
field, and because both halves are independently true — the metadata does date
the edit to 18 August, and Microsoft does say account updates begin that day.
Nothing here needs changing; I record it so a future round touching these
strings knows which clause is load-bearing.

## 5. The flake re-run was legitimate — judged, not just verified

The check went red once on the runner-launch test and green on an unedited
re-run. I judge the re-run defensible, on four independent grounds.

**The mechanism is real, pre-existing and already documented.**
`docket/open/2026-08-23-orchestrate-runner-launch-test-is-timing-dependent.md`
was filed by round 176 (build track), which observed four green runs and one
red on an *unchanged* tree. I confirmed the mechanism in the script rather than
taking the item's word: `scripts/test-orchestrate-runner-launch.mjs` sets
`setTimeout(…, 6000)` at line 250, SIGKILLs the child and resolves with
whatever output has accumulated, then at line 258 asserts `/iteration
starting/` matched. If the sandboxed child has not reached that line in 6000 ms,
the assertion falls through to a gate-failure report. The test genuinely cannot
distinguish "the gate blocked this" from "6 seconds was not enough", and
reports the first when it observed the second.

**The changed files cannot reach it.** I checked this from the diff rather than
accepting the claim. The deltas since the prior green runs are exactly
`CHANGELOG.md`, `app/lib/posts.js`, `app/blog/copilot-consolidation/page.js`
(plus my own review markdown under `docket/reviews/`). Grepping
`test-orchestrate-runner-launch.mjs` and `orchestrate.sh` for any reference to
`CHANGELOG`, `app/`, `posts.js`, `blog`, `sitemap`, `page-origins` or
`route-files` returns exactly one hit — a *comment* on line 12 of the test
pointing a reader at `CHANGELOG.md` for background. Neither file reads anything
this round changed. There is no path, direct or indirect, from a blog page or a
markdown entry to a sandboxed runner gate.

**A content-caused failure would be deterministic, and it was not.** I have now
run `node scripts/round.mjs check` four times across this round's trees — once
at `3577f16`, twice at `06ecf84`, once at `7bb5e86` — and the runner-launch
test passed in all four, including on this exact tree. Intermittency across
identical inputs is the signature of a timing flake, not of a broken tree.

**The round could not have fixed it, and did the next best thing.** The fix
lives in `scripts/`, outside `author` scope, so this round was correctly barred
from touching it — and under CHARTER rule 11 a run blocked by a guardrail may
file the case but not loosen it. The case is already filed. The round recorded
the occurrence in its Guardrails line and cited the item's own warning that
"re-run until green" is the habit that eventually waves a real failure through.

**That disclosure is the load-bearing part of my judgement.** "Re-run until
green" is defensible here only because all three of these hold at once: the
failure mode is documented with a mechanism, the changed files are provably
unrelated to it, and the occurrence was written down rather than quietly
discarded. Remove any one and I would not accept it. A future round citing this
review as precedent should be held to the same three conditions, not to "round
