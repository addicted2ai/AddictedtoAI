## The highest-value attack: two CDP drivers

Read `scripts/check-reflow.mjs` and the new `scripts/lib/cdp-browser.mjs`
line by line against each other. The round's own defence (disclosure #8)
holds up: `check-reflow.mjs` measures 320×800 with `mobile: true` and reads
`documentElement.clientWidth`/`scrollWidth`; `check-first-screenful.mjs`
(via the new library) measures 1280×800 with `mobile: false` and reads
`getBoundingClientRect()` on `li`/`tr` elements. Neither's assertion depends
on a quantity the other computes, and the mobile-emulation footgun the
`check-reflow.mjs` header warns about (`window.innerWidth` expanding to fit
overflow content) is specific to that file's own denominator choice — the
new probe never touches `innerWidth` or `clientWidth` at all, so it can't
inherit that bug.

The admitted gap is real, though: `measurePage()`'s wait-for-load logic
(`Page.loadEventFired` + fixed 400ms) is copied **verbatim, character for
character**, from `checkRoute()` in `check-reflow.mjs`. I confirmed there is
no test enforcing this (`scripts/test-*.mjs` has nothing for
`cdp-browser.mjs` or `check-first-screenful.mjs` — the full test list is
`test-tool-links-overflow`, `test-review-artifact`, `test-check-deployments`,
`test-orchestrate-checkout`, `test-peak-window`,
`test-dispatch-generative-push`, `test-check-reflow-known-failures`,
`test-orchestrate-hold`, `test-free-model-pattern`,
`test-orchestrate-runner-launch`, `test-runner-preflight`). If a future round
tunes `check-reflow.mjs`'s wait strategy (e.g. adds a network-idle wait
because a route starts needing one), `cdp-browser.mjs` will silently drift
out of sync with nothing to catch it. Worth filing as a follow-up; not a
defect in what shipped, since my own render-timing test (400ms vs 3000ms,
above) found no evidence the current wait is too short for any of the three
routes I checked.

## The measurement itself

The probe's definition (`FIRST_SCREENFUL_PROBE` in
`scripts/check-first-screenful.mjs`) is `li, tr` elements whose
`getBoundingClientRect()` satisfies `width > 2 && height > 2 && top < 800 &&
bottom > 0`, against a 1280×800 CDP viewport. That is the docket item's own
definition ("`tr` or `li` intersecting the first 800px of a 1280-wide
viewport"), not a looser or stricter one. The `> 2` size filter exists to
exclude the site's `.visually-hidden` 1×1px trick from counting as visible
content; it made no difference to any of the three routes I independently
re-measured (`noSizeFilter` matched `strict` in every case). Only one route
(`/model-retirement-calendar`) carries a blocking minimum in `ROUTES`; the
other six are measured and printed, never asserted — confirmed by reading
the exported map directly, matching disclosure #3.

## `app/directory/DirectorySearch.js` — real fix or metric bait?

Read the full file. The `<li>` wrapping touches only the render return value
— `matches()`, `filteredCategories`, the search input's `onChange`, the
debounced live-region announcement, and `key={tool.href}` (moved from the
`<a>` to its new `<li>` parent, still unique) are untouched. Nothing about
search, filtering, or keyboard interaction changed. Confirmed the claim that
`app/demos/ToolFinder.js`'s identically-named `.tool-grid`/`.tool-card`
classes are unaffected by the new CSS: that file's grid is a `<div>` with
`<a>` children directly (no `<li>`), so `.tool-grid > li { display: flex }`
matches nothing there, and `.tool-card { flex: 1 }` is inert on a grid item
that isn't inside a flex container (`flex` has no effect outside a flex
context). Screenshotted `/directory` at 1280×800 — card grid renders
identically to before, no visual regression.

On whether this is a genuine accessibility fix or metric gaming: both are
true, and the round says so itself rather than picking one. The markup
correction is real and independently defensible — a collection of same-kind
cards as bare `<a>` siblings of a `<div>` is a genuine "list of N items"
gap for screen readers, and the fix is inert everywhere else in the
codebase (verified above), not a special-cased hack. But the round also
disclosed it went looking specifically for a zero-count page where this
particular fix would be defensible, and that the homepage's `section-grid`
(`app/page.js` line 170: `<div className="section-grid">` wrapping bare
`<a className="section-card">` children) is, in its own words, "structurally
the same pattern" left untouched — confirmed by reading `app/page.js`
directly. The fix dominates as a real improvement; the selection process
was metric-driven. Both are disclosed, neither is hidden.
