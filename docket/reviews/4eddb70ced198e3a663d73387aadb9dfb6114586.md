## 2. The fifth instance, and the timezone arithmetic behind it

The round's report is accurate on both counts.

- **The line-break gap was real and my original review's grep would in fact
  have missed it.** At e8d3d9e, `page.js` read (line 56-57): `...CEST on 23`
  / `August, per Manus's own conversion.` — "23" and "August" on separate
  source lines. My original review ran `grep -n "23 August"
  app/lib/posts.js app/blog/manus-meta-split/page.js`, a single-line-pattern
  match, and it reported exactly 3 hits in `page.js` (lines 55, 67, 72) —
  none of them this one, because grep does not match a pattern split across
  a newline. JSX collapses that newline into a single space when rendered,
  so the browser shows "...CEST on 23 August, per..." as one continuous
  phrase — invisible to the check I ran, visible to a reader. Confirmed
  this really was a gap in my method, not a false claim by the round.
- **The dates added are the correct dates, not just present dates.** The
  fixed sentence reads: `"— that is 7:59 p.m. EDT on 22 August 2026, or
  1:59 a.m. CEST on 23 August 2026, per Manus's own conversion."` The
  deadline is 7:59 a.m. SGT on 23 August 2026 = 23:59 UTC on 22 August 2026.
  EDT is UTC−4: 23:59 − 4h = 19:59 EDT on 22 August 2026 = 7:59 p.m. EDT on
  22 August 2026 — matches. CEST is UTC+2: 23:59 + 2h = 01:59 CEST on 23
  August 2026 = 1:59 a.m. CEST on 23 August 2026 — matches. Both years are
  attached to the correct calendar day for their timezone, not swapped. This
  also still matches Manus's own live page (re-checked in the original
  review, unchanged since): `"...will run until 7:59 p.m. EDT on August 22 /
  1:59 a.m. CEST on August 23 / 7:59 a.m. SGT on August 23, 2026."` A
  confidently wrong conversion would have been worse than the original bare
  date — this one is right.
