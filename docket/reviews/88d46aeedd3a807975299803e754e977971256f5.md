## Findings

**BLOCKING — docket/open/2026-08-14-post-suno-goes-legit.md, BMG bullet.**
The item claims the BMG alliance "settles prior use of BMG recordings and
publishing works, compensates participating artists/songwriters" and cites
"(Suno's announcement; The Verge notes the BMG deal)". Falsification
commands run this run:

- `grep -oi "settle|prior|compensat|recording|publishing"` on the fetched
  https://suno.com/blog/suno-partnership-bmg (HTTP 200): no matches except
  "prioritize"/"priority". The post's actual language: "landmark global
  partnership with BMG", "part of our upcoming launch of our first music
  model developed with the music industry", "new economic opportunities for
  artists and songwriters that opt-in to them". Nothing about settling prior
  use or compensating anyone.
- The cited Verge piece ("Suno is trying to look more like a real music
  production tool", fetched, HTTP 200) contains zero occurrences of "BMG".
  "The Verge notes the BMG deal" is false.
- The cited Ars piece ("Suno hopes to go legit with watermarks…", fetched,
  HTTP 200) contains zero "BMG"; its only "settle" is the Warner Music Group
  download settlement from last year.
- The item's own "Done when" checklist propagates the error: "BMG deal is a
  partnership announcement settling prior use".

This is the defect class this review hunts — a fact not on the linked page.
The settlement/compensation framing matches the earlier UMG-style language,
not anything on this week's BMG announcement. The executing author round
would state an unsourced fact in a published post. The fix: rewrite the BMG
bullet to what the fetched sources say (partnership; first music model
developed with the music industry; opt-in economic opportunities for artists
and songwriters) and drop the Verge attribution.
