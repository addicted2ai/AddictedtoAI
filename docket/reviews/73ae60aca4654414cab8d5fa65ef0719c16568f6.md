## 1. Blocking — "All ten still hold word for word" is nine, and this commit made it so

**Where.** `app/what-vendors-promise/page.js:293-305` (new footnote):

> "every quoted sentence checked as a contiguous run of words in the page it

word**".

**What is wrong.** The restored OpenAI `sentence` in
`app/lib/retirement-commitments.js` concatenates the lead-in paragraph and all
three bullets, and silently drops the vendor's text *between* bullet two and
bullet three:

> "Examples include chat variants such as `gpt-5.1-chat-latest`, Codex
> variants such as `gpt-5.3-codex`, and deep research variants such as
> `o3-deep-research`."

It also drops bullet three's tail ("Examples include `computer-use-preview` and
`gpt-4o-audio-preview`. We don't recommend using preview models for
business-critical production workloads unless you can migrate on short
notice."). Neither elision is marked with an ellipsis, and `page.js:142` renders
the value inside `&ldquo;…&rdquo;`.

**Verified two ways, against the live page fetched this review.** Under the
round's own stated test — a contiguous run of *words*, punctuation and markup
discarded so that markup-boundary artifacts cannot cause a false negative — the
ten verified rows come out **9 contiguous, 1 not**:

```
FAIL not contiguous: OpenAI — matched 36 of 58 words
     quote continues: "preview models preview models identified by preview in the model name may be retired"
     page continues:  "examples include chat variants such as gpt-5 1-chat-latest codex variants such as gpt-5 3-codex"
OK  contiguous word run: Anthropic / Mistral / Amazon Bedrock / Microsoft Foundry /
    Alibaba / Google (Gemini API) / DeepSeek / xAI (Grok) / Cohere
SKIP (unverified): Meta (Llama)
contiguous: 9   not contiguous: 1
```

A first pass that kept punctuation reported Google, DeepSeek and xAI as
failures too; all three were artifacts of my own HTML-to-text conversion
inserting a space where a `<code>` tag had been — exactly the class of false
positive the round describes and discounts in change 4. Discounting them is
correct. The OpenAI failure is not of that class: it is 22 words of real vendor
prose, a whole sentence, skipped mid-list.

**This commit introduced it.** The pre-correction two-bullet quote *was* a
contiguous run of words in the page. That is why, as the round correctly says,
"a substring check had never flagged it". The corrected quote is the first
version that cannot pass the check the page advertises.

**Also.** The correction note itself says "The quote is restored in full
above." It is not restored in full — three example clauses and one
recommendation sentence remain elided.

**Why this is blocking and not a wording note.** It is a published count that
does not reproduce (ten → nine), it is the evidentiary basis offered for ten
verification stamps, and it appears on a page whose whole subject is vendors
who state part of a commitment as though it were the whole of it. The round is
right that quoting two of three bullets misled a reader; presenting an elided
concatenation inside quotation marks as "word for word" is the same shape of
claim, one level down.

**Fix — one line, touching nothing else verified below.** Either mark the
elisions in `sentence` (a `…` between bullet two and bullet three, and after
"such as 2 weeks") and keep the footnote claim, or restate the footnote and the
changelog to say nine of ten quotes are contiguous runs and the OpenAI row is
that page's three-bullet list with its example clauses elided. The first is
preferable: it keeps the check honest for every future round.
