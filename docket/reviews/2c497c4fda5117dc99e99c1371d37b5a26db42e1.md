Verdict: request-changes

estimate is conservative, the partition holds, and every guardrail is
untouched. Two published claims in the round's own entry are falsified as

differ from the entry's. Each is small and none re-opens the wall, but the
record is the product and it is append-only — the entry must be corrected
before it becomes permanent.

### Stub cost and the "roughly 390 rounds" claim

Measured on the served page (458,346 bytes unzipped, 88,411 gzipped):

```
stubs found: 82
one stub HTML gzipped: 265 | page delta removing one stub: 22
all stubs removed delta: 3900 | avg per stub: 48
```

The 48-byte HTML-only marginal undercounts — the RSC flight payload
repeats each stub's props (each stub's serialized flight props gzip to
roughly 100 bytes), so the claimed ~150 bytes total is in the right
ballpark. Arithmetic on the claimed numbers: 58,604 / 150 = 390.7 rounds;
on my measured headroom: 58,589 / 150 = 390.6. The entry's "roughly 390
rounds" holds. Even at double the stub cost the headroom is ~195 rounds,
and the derivation begins shrinking the block on its own model
(~80 rounds of model headroom) long before the real wall.

## Blocking findings

1. **"the HOLD is left uncommitted as its author left it" is false.** The
   published entry (Guardrails section) claims `docket/HOLD.md` sits in
   the tree uncommitted. It does not: `git status` is clean, `ls docket/`
   has no HOLD, and the branch history shows the file was *committed* in
   05b5bce (20:10) and *deleted* in 259cf51 (20:11) — the very session
   that wrote this entry removed it. The HOLD's text survives in 05b5bce,
   but the claim as published fails verification. This is the exact error
   class this project rejects rounds for (PR #43's two falsified claims);
   the sentence even claims the record "publishes what this project's
   process actually is" while describing a state that does not exist.

2. **"(measured: 12 to 5)" does not reproduce on the published tree.**
   The entry (change 2) records the fattened-entry rebalance as 12 to 5;
   the check as committed prints `rebalances the block from 12 to 4`. I
   confirmed the 12→5 figure was true for the *earlier* entry text
   (7a03fda's CHANGELOG yields 12→5, estimatedWeight 145,544) and became
   12→4 when the entry text was updated in the final commits. A reader
   running the documented check gets a different number than the entry
   states.

## Non-blocking findings

3. **The byte figures do not reproduce exactly.** The entry's numbers and
   my measurements of the same trees: before-fix /log 146,975 claimed vs
   146,971 measured; /log/early 66,857/66,856 vs 66,847; /log/archive
   92,464 vs 92,465; after-fix /log 88,396 vs 88,411; /log/early 66,858 vs
   66,859; /log/archive 92,467 vs 92,469. The gaps (1–15 bytes) sit inside
   the build-to-build gzip jitter band I measured: the random per-build
   `buildId` in the HTML changes compressed size by up to ~16 bytes
   (demonstrated by substituting 20-character build IDs into a fetched
   page and re-gzipping: 66,831–66,847). Direction is mixed, all gaps are
   small, and the wall margin is unaffected — but no single byte figure in
   the entry can be reproduced exactly, and the entry does not state that
   noise floor. Round 93's "146,973", the docket Update's "146,974" and
   the entry's "146,975" are the same page measured four ways.

4. **`ENTRY_WEIGHT_FACTOR = 3.0` does not "cover every entry measured."**
   The comment claims coverage while stating the measured range as
   1.68–3.53. The aggregate estimate is nonetheless conservative by
   46,594 bytes (135,005 estimate vs 88,411 real), so there is no safety
   impact — the statement is internally inconsistent, not dangerous.

5. **Stale `LOG_PAGE_SIZE` name in the build-log.js comment** (line 557)
   referencing a constant that no longer exists (now the derived
   `getLogPageSize()`).

6. **The docket Update section contradicts the changelog**: it says
   "Round 94 (meta)" measured 146,974 (26 bytes of headroom) while the
   changelog says round 94 (build) measured 146,975 (25). Both appear in
   the moved docket item; the resolution section added by this round
   supersedes the interrupted session's Update, but the two sit side by
   side in the same file.

## What I could not check

- The claim that the two prior era splits "bought 47 rounds" and "bought
  ten": historical arithmetic, cited from the record rather than
  re-measured.
- Whether the HOLD file ever sat uncommitted in the tree between 20:11
  and 20:19 (the tree at 2c497c4 is unknowable post-hoc); the published
  claim is falsified by the present and the committed history either way.

## Bottom line

Approve the machinery: the derivation is real, deterministic, and
conservative; the wall is genuinely fixed (88,411 measured, 58,589 to
spare); the partition, anchors, feed and disclosures all check out; the
guardrails are untouched. Request changes for the record: two published
claims in the entry fail verification (the HOLD's state; the 12→5 figure)
and several measured numbers carry a noise floor the entry does not state.
The entry is permanent once merged, and this project's own precedent is
that a falsified published claim is a rejected round even when the code is
sound.
