## Summary

calculation, and archive capture I re-fetched independently matches what the
post says it matches. But the post is a public accusation-shaped piece about
a named company's conduct against a statute, and its structure — not any

it believing OpenAI is in breach, that is BLOCKING and you must name the
passages that produce it." I name them below.

## What did not survive — the blocking finding

Reading the post as a reader (headline, lead, section order — not a
clause-by-clause parse), three structural things combine to leave the
impression that OpenAI is short of a legal duty, before the disclaimers that
would correct it ever appear:

1. **The caveats are last.** "What this post does not determine" — the
   section carrying "no compliance determination," "whether OpenAI is a
   covered provider was not verified," and "no enforcement action is
   known" — is the second-to-last section, after: the statute's penalty
   figures, the "gap is not abstract" lead, an entire section titled
   "Sora's video is marked. OpenAI's own tool cannot check it," and a
   section that measures how many hours before the deadline OpenAI shipped
   audio support. A reader who reads the headline and lead — which is most
   of them — never reaches the caveat that the post makes no compliance
   determination. That is "a hedge buried below the accusation," which the
   brief says is decoration, not a caveat.

2. **The timing section implies motive despite disclaiming it.** "When
   audio was added, and how close to the deadline" measures the gap between
   OpenAI shipping audio support and the statute's operative date down to
   the minute, in two timezones. It closes with "this post does not know,
   and does not guess, why OpenAI shipped it when it did." But the section's
   existence, its title, and the precision of the arithmetic all do the
   work of implying OpenAI was racing a deadline it recognized applied to
   it — which is precisely the "cumulative rhetoric... the timeline" the
   brief told me to weigh, independent of the sentence that disclaims it.
   You cannot structurally center a race-the-clock narrative and then
   disclaim the inference it invites; the invitation survives the
   disclaimer.

3. **The closing sentence of the Sora section quietly re-tightens "or" back
   toward "and."** Section 1 earns real credit for stating, explicitly:
   "That is *or*, not *and* — a tool does not need to cover all three to be
   aimed at by the statute's wording." Under that reading, OpenAI's verifier
   covering images and audio already satisfies criterion (1) regardless of
   video, which — if taken seriously — deflates the entire "gap" framing as
   a compliance concern. But three sections later, the Sora section closes
   with: "Whether that satisfies a statute that names video as one of three
   formats **a free tool must cover** is a legal question this post does
   not answer." "A free tool must cover [video]" reads as exactly the
   all-three framing the post explicitly rejected earlier, restated at the
   rhetorical climax of the piece, right next to the section header "OpenAI's
   own tool cannot check it." This is not the literal "or"-to-"AND" swap
   round 190's review caught in an earlier draft's title, but it is the same
   defect in substance: a sentence that treats video coverage as required
   when the post's own statutory reading, three sections earlier, said it
   is not.

None of this is a fabricated fact. All three are the brief's failure mode 2:
"no individual fact is wrong, but the post as a whole tells a reader that
OpenAI is breaking the law." On the "does the post rely on the
covered-provider question while claiming not to" test the brief sets: the
post's own rhetorical weight — leading with the $5,000-per-day penalty
figures, headlining the video gap, measuring the deadline race, titling a
section "OpenAI's own tool cannot check it" — only makes sense to a reader
if OpenAI is assumed to be bound by the statute. The disclaimer that this
was never established arrives after all of that work is done.

**What I'd ask for, concretely:** move the "no compliance determination" and
"covered-provider status not verified" caveats into or immediately after the
lead paragraph, not four sections later. Fix the closing Sora-section
sentence so it doesn't restate an all-three-required framing the post
already refuted. Consider whether the timing section needs its own explicit
"a reader might infer OpenAI was responding to this deadline; this post does
not draw that inference and the record does not support it" line, stated
where the timing claim is made, not only in the caveats section.
