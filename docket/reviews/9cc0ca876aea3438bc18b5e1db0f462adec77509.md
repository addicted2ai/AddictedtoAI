## 2. The third note: right, not over-marking

Read it as a reader, weighing both sides the brief raised.

The case for marking it: "Sol's pricing is unchanged," in "What the prices
were, and what they are now," sits in the same sentence-level present tense
as "Terra is $2... and Luna is $0.20..." — both still true today. Nothing in
the clause itself (unlike line 74's launch-price sentence, which opens with
"When the GPT-5.6 family launched on 9 July, OpenAI priced... at," an
unambiguous historical frame) marks it as bound to the 30-July announcement
rather than to today. A reader skimming for "what does Sol cost" who lands
here first — plausible; it's the first pricing section on the page — gets
the stale answer with no cue that this specific clause, unlike its neighbors
in the same sentence, has gone false.

The case against: the paragraph opens "Three weeks later, on 30 July, OpenAI
published the cuts. Starting that day: ..." — a reader who carries that frame
through the whole paragraph could read "Sol's pricing is unchanged" as
"unchanged by the 30 July cuts specifically," which remains true and isn't
the same claim as "unchanged today." That reading is available, but it
requires the reader to actively carry a five-sentence-old temporal frame
through a flat, independent declarative sentence with no restated anchor —
a weaker defense than line 74's, where the anchor is in the same clause.

I land on: keep it. The bar this project set for itself is not "a defensible
reading exists" but "a reader is not misled," and the shared-tense
similarity to two still-accurate neighbors is a real trap regardless of
what a careful reader could reconstruct. The note itself is proportionate to
that risk, not heavy-handed: two sentences, no restated numbers (correctly —
it points to the fuller note below rather than triplicating the same
explanation), visually identical to the other two. Three notes across a
~250-line post, each guarding a distinct location where the same error could
mislead a reader arriving independently, is not the "volume that serves
process rather than a reader" the maintainer has flagged elsewhere — it's
proportionate to three real, distinct entry points into the same page. If
this were four or five notes, or if any one of them read as boilerplate
rather than addressing a specific local ambiguity, I'd call it over-marking.
It isn't, here.
