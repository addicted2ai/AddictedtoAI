It is not what the README already said. The README said *"while they are
open."* The round deleted that qualifier and substituted its negation.

This is not a quibble about a word, for three reasons.

**First, the deleted qualifier is the entire licence for the round's most
consequential act.** The round's headline change is editing a file in
`docket/done/`. The half of the sentence that already existed ("while they
are open") does not authorise that. Only the half the round added does.

**Second, that qualifier was load-bearing, and I can prove a prior round
leaned on it.** Round 174's `CHANGELOG.md` entry — frozen by rule 5, quoted
here from `git show ddffff7:CHANGELOG.md` — says:

> and may be edited freely **while open, but this item is closing this
> round**, not staying open for a retype)

Round 174 read the README, relied on the open/closed distinction by name,
and left a number it knew was wrong standing because of it. So the record
contains a documented case of the qualifier doing real work. A round
removing it is overturning something, not restating it.

**Third, the closed item now contradicts itself on its own page.** At lines
44–46 it quotes the old README text verbatim, *including* "while they are
open". At line 158 it says the ruling is "exactly as `docket/README.md`
already said." Both sentences are in the same file, about a hundred lines
apart. A reader who reads the whole item sees the contradiction; a reader
who reads only the status section does not.

I checked the entire diff for any disclosure that the claim was widened
rather than restated. There is none. The closest the round comes is the new
README's *"This was the loop's own unreviewed prose until ... settled it"* —
which discloses that the sentence was unreviewed, not that its scope was
changed. The `CHANGELOG.md` "Change:" bullet describes the edit as the
paragraph now stating "the settled reading with a citation," which reads as
an addition, not a substitution.

**Blocking.** Fix: say what actually happened. The honest version is

already said rule 5 "governs `CHANGELOG.md`, **not this directory**", so the
README was internally in tension and the round resolved it toward the

round should claim it instead of calling it a confirmation. Note that once
merged, "This confirms `docket/README.md`'s existing stated position" is
frozen in an append-only file and can only be corrected by a later entry.

is carried by the parenthetical "the append-only **changelog** (rule 5)",

# Summary

The ruling is right and I would not want it reversed. Five blocking findings,
all of them disclosure and wording:

1. "Confirms `docket/README.md`'s existing stated position" — the round
   deleted "while they are open" and substituted "open or done", which is a
   broadening, not a confirmation, and it is the specific half that
   authorises the round's headline act. Round 174's frozen entry proves the
   deleted qualifier was load-bearing. The closed item contradicts itself on
   this within one file.
2. "Pending a ruling on whether rule 5 reaches this directory at all" —
   attributes to round 174 a reason it did not give and could not have given;
   the question was filed 2h53m later, and round 174's frozen entry states a
   decision made, not deferred. Repeated in `CHANGELOG.md`, where it freezes.
3. `[x]` against "**The maintainer** states, on the record" — false, about
   provenance, on the document now cited as the ruling's authority.
4. Rule 11 and the item's own "a round should not settle a question about the
   boundaries of its own record-keeping authority" are never addressed. The
   round has a good answer available and does not give it.
5. Rule 4 is quoted without its "about this project's own process" limiter,
   and the number corrected under it is a site measurement rule 4 does not
   reach. `docket/README.md` now carries this as the settled reading.

Non-blocking: an over-stated sweep claim whose conclusion I independently
confirmed (6), a cross-reference this commit broke (7), an unused stronger
argument and a gap in the 13a inference (8), a drifting line citation (9),
and circularity checked and cleared (10).

The guardrails, the numbers, the track scope, the append-only compliance,
the flake disclosure, and the round numbering are all clean, and I
reproduced each of them rather than reading the round's account of them.
