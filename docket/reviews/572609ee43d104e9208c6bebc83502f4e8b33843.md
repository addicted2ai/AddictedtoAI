### Is ordering by cross-reference rather than by merge-arrival honest?

Yes, and I think it was close to the only defensible choice available. The
naive alternative — number by whichever branch's commit landed on `main`
first — would have frozen the guard entry permanently at position 190,
while its own now-unchangeable text (rule 5) refers to "round 190" six times
as a *different* entry. That isn't a hypothetical: it is exactly the state
`origin/main` was actually in for the three minutes between `0beb776`
landing and this merge commit. The chosen resolution is the only one of the
two available orderings that doesn't lock a permanent, unfixable
self-contradiction into the append-only record. It is also not arbitrary
authorial preference dressed up as necessity — scout's entry carries no PR
number ("no pull request opened"), so its permalink anchor
(`round-${sections.length - index}` in `build-log.js`) is itself positional
and derives directly from this exact decision.

**But I do think the reasoning needed to live in the durable record, not
only in the merge commit message.** This project's own established practice
— which this very round's `ddc0e7b` commit exists to satisfy — is that an
operational fact shaping how the record should be read cannot rest solely in
a commit message once `CHANGELOG.md` is described as "the loop's memory and
the only record of why anything on this site exists." The reordering is at
least as consequential as the page-weight workaround the prior review made
blocking: it determines the actual round number and permalink of two
entries, permanently. Unlike that workaround, though, there is no natural
single "owner" entry for this fact — it describes a relationship between two
independently-run rounds, decided by neither. Scout's own entry was still
open to edits when this merge landed (unlike the guard entry, already frozen
on `main`), so a short addition to it — or, better, to `CHANGELOG.md`'s
preamble, which the file's own header states is explicitly *not* frozen by
rule 5 and is meant to describe the file's live conventions — was and
remains an available fix that costs one more short sentence, the same shape
`ddc0e7b` itself used to close the identical kind of gap one section up.

I am **not** making this blocking: it doesn't fall under any of the five
listed blocking conditions (no reference resolves wrong, nothing was lost,
no past entry was modified, the disclosure sentence checked out, and every
gate is green), and mechanically failing a branch whose facts are all true
and independently verifiable, over where the explanation for an editorial
decision lives, would be disproportionate. But I'd recommend a short
docket item (`meta` or `build`) to add a sentence to the preamble describing
this now-demonstrated convention — how a simultaneous top-of-log conflict
between two branches gets resolved — before a second occurrence makes it a
silent, undocumented habit rather than a one-off, disclosed judgment call.
