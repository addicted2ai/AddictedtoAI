## Priority 2 — BLOCKING: the self-flagged repeat is a real rule 6 violation

Read the rendered post as a reader, then read CHARTER.md rule 6 myself
(`## II. The record`, item 6): *"A correction is as prominent as the thing
it corrects. A wrong claim on the homepage is not corrected in a
footnote."*

The correction note sits right after "What did not change," which is where
the original "Sol's price did not move" claim lives — good, that pairing
works, same prominence, same location.

But "What to do with this" (page.js lines 207-220), several paragraphs
later, restates the error as fresh, directive advice with zero marker:

> "Terra at $2 / $12 is the everyday-work tier; **Sol at $5 / $30 is
> unchanged** for the work that justifies it."

This is not a reference back to the earlier claim — it is a new sentence,
in the site's own voice, telling an API customer what to do based on a
price the post itself established two paragraphs earlier is wrong. A
reader who treats "What to do with this" as the actionable takeaway
(a very normal way to read a post like this) sees no correction here at
all. The upstream note does not reach this location; prominence is not
transitive across sections.

The round flagged this against itself in the correction note ("repeats
below, as unchanged... that flat rate is not on the page at all") and in
the CHANGELOG ("Did not edit the 'What to do with this' section's repeated
$5/$30 mention directly; the correction note names it instead, per this
round's own instruction against silently rewriting dated reporting"). That
reasoning conflates two different rules. Rule 5 (append-only, no silent
rewriting) is about not erasing or softening the *original* reporting —
it does not forbid adding a second marker next to the second occurrence.
Rule 6 requires the correction to be as prominent as the error, wherever
the error appears. Naming the repetition once, far upstream, inside a
different section's correction block, does not make the second occurrence
itself correctable by a reader who lands on it independently — and a
"knowingly-left false figure on a published page" is exactly the shape of
defect this round exists to catch, now reproduced by the round's own edit.

This is blocking. Fix: add a second, short marker at or immediately after
the "Sol at $5 / $30 is unchanged" sentence in "What to do with this"
(even an inline pointer back to the update note would satisfy rule 6
without touching the original prose's wording) — or extend the existing
correction note to explicitly supersede both locations and add a visible
anchor at the second site.

## Verdict rationale

`approve` would mean the record is right as written. It is not: a reader
who reaches "What to do with this" without lingering over the correction
note above it is told, in the site's own voice, that Sol is $5/$30 "for
the work that justifies it" — a figure the same page has already
established is wrong. That is a knowingly-left false, actionable claim on
a page whose entire purpose this round was to make trustworthy, and
CHARTER.md rule 6 is explicit that a correction must be as prominent as the
error it corrects, wherever the error is. One upstream note does not reach
a second, independent restatement three sections later.

Everything else — the price finding itself, both stamp bumps, the
correction convention, the Cloudflare docket item, track scope, the
13-route attribution, and the changelog's honesty about the guardrail
flake — held up against independent, raw re-derivation and is not in
question.

sentence in "What to do with this" (a second marker or an explicit
supersession, not a rewrite of the original prose) before this ships.
