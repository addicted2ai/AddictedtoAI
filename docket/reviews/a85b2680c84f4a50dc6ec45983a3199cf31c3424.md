  "measured this round." or "measured this round," — with a period or
  comma, not the entry's stated em-dash form. The entry's rule text says
  "opens 'measured this run —' or 'measured this round —'", and only 17 of
  the 26 openings literally carry an em dash. The count only reproduces
  when the rule is read as "measured this run/round" plus any punctuation
  — a reading the entry's own quoted examples (104 with comma, 116 with
  period) demonstrate, so a reader can verify the number, but the literal
  em-dash sentence alone would yield 17. Same looseness for the caveat:
  "appends a 'Not yet measured:' / 'Not measured:' caveat clause" is
  literally true of only 17 of the 20; rounds 104 ("…is not yet
  measured."), 107 ("…was not measured and the post says…") and 108

  claim.") append the caveat in non-colon prose. The 20/6 split holds
  exactly under the phrase-presence rule the entry states two sentences

  visible two sentences from the shorthand — so I do not judge either a
  falsified number, but a stricter writer would have written "opens
  'measured this run/round'" and "a not-measured caveat clause".
