## 3. Is the new closing line true, and doing the right work?

> "That gap does not, on its own, mean OpenAI's tools fall short of the law
> above — criterion (1) is written as *image, video, or audio*, not all
> three, exactly as this post read it earlier. What is checkable,
> independent of that legal question, is narrower and stands on its own:
> OpenAI's public tool does not reach the one format its own newest
> generative product makes."

Checked against the statute text I fetched and quoted verbatim in my first
pass: §22757.2(a)(1) reads "The tool allows a user to assess whether image,
video, or audio content, or content that is any combination thereof, was
created or altered by the covered provider's GenAI system." Under that
wording, a tool covering images and audio already satisfies criterion (1)
regardless of video — "or" means any one (or any combination) suffices. So
"that gap does not, on its own, mean OpenAI's tools fall short of the law
above" is **true**, and it is the correct, careful statement: it says the
video gap doesn't *by itself* establish a shortfall (leaving room for the
covered-provider question and the other five criteria, neither of which
this post evaluates), not that the gap doesn't matter at all. The second
sentence catches the fall precisely at "OpenAI's public tool does not reach
the one format its own newest generative product makes" — a claim that
needs no legal predicate to be true and interesting. This is the calibration
the brief asked for: accurate about what the statute's "or" does and
doesn't establish, without swinging to "so none of this matters."
