The round's own working notes (not in the committed diff, but described in
my brief) flagged that "one command" was not found verbatim on the pages
it fetched and that it bumped the stamp anyway on an inference from
"well-established product behavior." That is exactly the shape of defect
this whole line of work exists to catch, so I fetched the pages myself
rather than take the round's self-doubt or its committed comment at face
value.

Raw `curl` (full browser UA, and separately with default UA and OS-spoofed
UAs for mac/linux/windows) against `https://ollama.com` and
`https://ollama.com/download` (plus `/download/linux` and `/download/mac`
explicitly):

- `ollama.com` homepage: `<h1>Build with open models,<br />on your computer
  and in the cloud</h1>` and, lower on the page, `"Cloud models in the
  United States, Europe, and Singapore."` — verbatim support for both the
  "or in the cloud" clause and the round's "cloud regions" claim.
- `ollama.com/download` (every UA variant I tried, including the bare
  default `curl` UA, which lands on the macOS tab by default): the page's
  headline install method is a single copy-paste command —
  `curl -fsSL https://ollama.com/install.sh | sh` on the mac/Linux tabs,
  `irm https://ollama.com/install.ps1 | iex` on the Windows tab — each
  shown in a `<code class="command">` block with a copy button, ahead of
  the alternate `.exe`/`.dmg` download link.

This is not a paraphrase read into the page — a single command is
literally the primary, headlined install path on every OS variant of the
one page the round says it fetched. "Run open-source LLMs locally with one
command" is directly supportable from what is actually there. Per the
brief's own framing: a description is not a quotation, and the round was
over-scrupulous in treating "no exact substring match" as "unverified."
**My explicit call: the Ollama stamp is earned.** The round should not
have needed to fall back to "well-established product behavior" — the
substance was on the page it already fetched, and flagging otherwise was
the round being more cautious than the facts required, not a real gap.
