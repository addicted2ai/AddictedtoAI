## Task 2: is 1 the right floor, or has fact 2 been weakened past usefulness

I don't think it's been weakened. The claim fact 2 makes, after the
narrowing an earlier review pass already forced onto it, is specifically
"the file's commit history, as merged into `main`, has not been deleted" —
not "a specific count of hold/release cycles has occurred." Read against
that claim, a floor of 1 is not a compromise, it's the literal, correct
threshold: history that has not been deleted has a count greater than
zero, full stop. I confirmed the check still fails at the case that
actually matters — `count=0` — trivially, since `[ "$count" -ge 1 ]` is
false there; that's the only scenario "deleted" can honestly mean for an
append-only record this check has any way to observe.

The more interesting question is why *not* pick something between 1 and
10 — say, 4, matching today's honest CI number. I think that would be the
wrong choice, and for the same reason 10 was wrong: 4 is exactly as much a
snapshot of one moment's ref-visibility as 10 was, just a smaller one.
Nothing about the underlying claim ("has this ever been used, and does
that history survive") requires the count to stay at or above whatever
`origin/main` happens to hold today — future hold/release cycles will only
grow it, but hardcoding today's number as a floor risks the identical bug
class this exact episode just found: a check whose passing threshold
depends on incidental facts about *when* and *from what ref-visibility* it
runs, rather than on the property it claims to verify. A floor of 1 is the
only value that is invariant to that — it is satisfied the moment the
mechanism has been used even once and stays satisfied for the rest of the
project's life, which is what "has not been deleted" actually requires and
nothing more. I don't read this as "lowering a threshold until a check
passes"; the check was never really measuring what its old floor implied,
and this fix aligns the number with the narrower, already-agreed claim
rather than inventing new scope for the check to grow into.

I'll note one adjacent, out-of-scope observation rather than a finding:
floor=1 cannot catch *partial* history rewriting (nine of ten real commits
force-deleted, one left standing). I don't think that's a gap in this
check so much as a reminder that record-integrity and this-mechanism-exists
are different concerns this project already keeps separate elsewhere — the
append-only guarantee itself is tracked as its own, already-filed, already
disclosed gap (`docket/open/2026-08-13-changelog-append-only-unenforced.md`,
found in an earlier pass of this same review), not something fact 2 was
ever meant to provide.
