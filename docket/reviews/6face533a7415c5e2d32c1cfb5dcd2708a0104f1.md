## Finding: the corrected rationale is false for four of the four selectors it names, not just the one it fixed

The round rewrote the `.log-lead` claim correctly — checked, `.log-lead` is
now accurately described as the exception, and `grep -rn "<article"
app/log/` (which the round's own new text cites) does return no matches,
confirming `/log`, `/log/early`, `/log/archive` have no `<article>` wrapper.
That part of the correction is accurate and matches what I found.

But the sentence built around it introduces a new, equally checkable claim
that the correction never verified: **"most of them cap secondary text
sitting alongside other content on a page built from `<article>` — a
homepage intro line, a per-entry field, a caption"** (`app/globals.css`,
near-identical wording in `CHANGELOG.md` and the docket item). This names
three of the four remaining selectors by their role — `.hero-lead` (homepage
intro line), `.log-field`/`.log-note` (per-entry field), `.walkthrough-caption`
(caption) — and asserts each sits on a page built from `<article>`.

I ran the same test the round's own text now cites, exhaustively rather than
on the one selector it happened to check:

    grep -rln 'className="hero-lead"|className="walkthrough-caption"|className="log-field"|className="log-note"' app --include=*.js
      app/demos/RoundWalkthrough.js   (.walkthrough-caption)
      app/log/LogEntry.js             (.log-field, .log-note)
      app/page.js                     (.hero-lead)

    grep -rl '<article' app
      19 files -- app/page.js, app/demos/RoundWalkthrough.js (or its host,
      app/demos/page.js), and app/log/LogEntry.js's four host pages
      (/log, /log/early, /log/archive, /log/rounds/[id]) are NOT among them.

Every one of `.hero-lead`, `.walkthrough-caption`, `.log-field`, and
`.log-note` is used in exactly one file each, and none of those three files
— nor any page that renders them — has an `<article>` wrapper anywhere.
`app/page.js` (the homepage) is a plain `<div>`; six `.hero-lead` paragraphs
are its primary prose, interleaved with a `<dl>` stats block, not secondary
text beside a wider `article p` column. `/demos` is a plain `<div>`; its one
`.walkthrough-caption` is inside a demo widget, not on an article page.
`/log`, `/log/early`, `/log/archive`, and `/log/rounds/[id]` are all plain
`<div>`s — the same finding my prior review made for `.log-lead` applies
identically to `.log-field`/`.log-note` on those same four routes, which the
correction does not mention at all.

So "most of them" — the four selectors this sentence explicitly covers —
fails the stated test 4 for 4, not 0 for 4 as the correction implies by
contrast with the named `.log-lead` exception. Only `.log-lead` itself ever
appears on an `<article>` page (`/charter`, `/model-retirement-calendar`,
confirmed by grep both times), which is exactly backwards from how the
correction frames it: `.log-lead` is presented as the outlier that breaks a
pattern the other four fit, when in fact `.log-lead` is the *only* one of
the five that ever fits the pattern at all.

This is not a smaller-scale repeat of the original defect — it is the same
category of error (a categorical claim about page structure, asserted
without checking it against the file tree), now stated with more specificity
(naming a concrete, testable mechanism — the `<article>` wrapper — and three
concrete role-examples) and repeated consistently across all three places
that carry the rationale (`app/globals.css`'s comment, the CHANGELOG entry,
and the closed docket item). It is also explicitly presented as something
"adversarial review found," which is accurate for the `.log-lead` half and
inaccurate framing for the other half — a reader has no way to tell, from
the text alone, that only one of the five claims was actually checked.
