## CHARTER.md rule 5 (append-only) — attacked directly

The entry under review is edited in place rather than corrected via a new
dated entry, which on its face looks like exactly what rule 5 forbids:
"No past entry is rewritten... Corrections are new entries that name what
they correct." I went looking for whether this is a real violation.

The entry being edited was **never published**. It was committed locally as
`6bda4ff` on 2026-08-18 and never pushed; the branch was rebased
(`826b7fe`) and then corrected (`c01bb9f6`) entirely pre-merge, on a branch
that has not yet reached `main`. `git diff origin/main..HEAD` (above) proves
nothing on `main` is touched — this is the entry's first appearance in the
public record, not a rewrite of one readers have already seen.

I checked for precedent rather than relying on that argument alone. Commit
`8d18a0d` ("correct the snapshot-lag figure in the round-160 entry")
directly edited round 160's still-unmerged entry in place — changing "four
days" to "34 rounds and 54 hours (2.25 days...)" — after review artifact
`docket/reviews/365f62e....md` rejected the branch for that exact number.
That in-place, pre-merge correction was itself reviewed and approved
(`docket/reviews/8d18a0d....md`, `Verdict: approve`) without rule 5 being
raised as a problem, and it merged as `d5ebb72` (#125). By contrast, when I
searched the log for corrections to entries that *were* already on `main`
(e.g. `CHANGELOG.md:2754` and `:3030`, both round 138 correcting rounds
136/137), the pattern is exactly what rule 5 requires: a **new** entry,
naming what it corrects, with the old entry's text left alone. Round 137's
own entry (`CHANGELOG.md:2919-2932`) states the distinction explicitly:
"The round-137 changelog entry and the docket item's evidence sections were
left untouched (rule 5, append-only); only the unticked box and the new
status note changed."

So the project's own practice draws the line at merge, not at commit: rule 5
binds published entries; a not-yet-merged entry on an open branch is still
being drafted, and this round's in-place correction follows the same shape
as the round-160 precedent, not a rule-5 violation. I could not find a
counter-example where a not-yet-merged entry was required to spawn a
separate correction entry instead of being fixed in place. Passes.
