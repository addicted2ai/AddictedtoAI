- **The denominator moved 11 → 9.** `/log`'s window is a derived byte budget,

  the *identical* error the round correctly diagnoses in round 188 one paragraph
  earlier: "the one match is round 188's own entry, which contains the word only
  because it is the entry reporting that the preset matched nothing." Round 189
  caught round 188 standing on its own entry and then stood on its own.

accessibility" while the entry beside it says 1 of 11. That is the failure mode
`app/lib/build-log.js:555` already documents against this project — *"28 rounds
say wrong" opened a page reporting 15* — reproduced in the record rather than
the panel. `prompts/shared/review.md` names it directly: "a page-size
measurement changes when the entry describing it is edited."

**What holds:** the correction's *direction* and its *mechanism* are right. Round
188's "0 of 11" genuinely does not reproduce; at base it is 1, and the single
match genuinely is 188's own entry. `/log/early` **1 of 23** and `/log/archive`
**11 of 47** reproduce exactly, both ways, on both trees — those two numbers are
sound and the `/log/early` case is, as the round argues, the stronger finding.

**Fix:** re-measure `/log` after the entry is final and publish 9 / 7 / 3 / 5 /
**2**, or state the figure as of a named commit. The "returns to zero once 188

the window, so it returns to zero only when both age out.

## The vendor claim — held to a news-fact-checking standard, and it passes

I fetched `https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations` myself rather
than reading the round's account of it. Every one of the five dates exists and
says what the round says it says. The fine-tuning section reproduces as:

    | May 7, 2026  | Creating fine-tuning jobs or training is not available to
    |              | organizations that have not previously run fine-tuning.
    | July 2, 2026 | Creating fine-tuning jobs is no longer available to
    |              | organizations that have not run inference on a fine-tuned
    |              | model in the past 60 days.
    | Jan 6, 2027  | Active existing customers will no longer be able to create
    |              | new fine-tuning jobs on this date. Inference on fine-tuned
    |              | models will be disabled only when the underlying base model
    |              | is deprecated.

Both sentences the decision turns on are verbatim on the page: *"Inference on
fine-tuned models will continue to be available until the base models are
deprecated"* and *"Inference on fine-tuned models will be disabled only when the
underlying base model is deprecated."* The Evals milestones likewise: 2026-10-31
*"Existing evals become read-only"*, and 2026-11-30 *"The Evals dashboard and API
are scheduled to shut down."*

- All four excluded dates exist, with the wording quoted. ✓
- The 2026-05-07 gloss ("closed to orgs that never fine-tuned") and the
  2026-07-02 gloss ("orgs with no fine-tuned inference in 60 days") are both
  faithful compressions, not distortions. ✓
- The 2026-11-30 shutdown **differs in kind** from the 2026-10-31 read-only
  milestone: one is the platform being switched off, the other is a write
  restriction on a platform that keeps serving. ✓
- **The rule sorts all five the way the round claims.** "A row is a date on
  which something stops working; a dated milestone that restricts a capability
  while what is already running keeps running is not a row" puts 2026-05-07,
  2026-07-02, 2026-10-31 and 2027-01-06 out (the vendor's own inference sentence
  settles three of them outright) and keeps 2026-11-30 in, which matches
  `retirement-dates.js:152`. I could not construct a reading on which it
  misclassifies one. ✓

On whether it is mechanical enough to stop the silent re-deciding: **mostly, and
enough.** For the three fine-tuning dates the vendor supplies the sentence that
decides it, so a future round applies the rule rather than re-deriving it — which
is what the item asked for. The read-only case still needs one judgement ("does
a read-only eval count as still running?"), and the header answers it in advance
with the worked example *"A read-only eval still opens"*, which is the right way
to handle a residual judgement. The closing paragraph naming exactly what a
future round would have to change, in the open, is the part that actually ends
the re-deciding.
