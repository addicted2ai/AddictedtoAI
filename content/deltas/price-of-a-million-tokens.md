These two figures sit at opposite ends of OpenAI's range, and the roughly
150-fold gap between them is not a like-for-like price cut. GPT-4 was the
dearest model OpenAI sold on the day it launched; gpt-5-nano is the
cheapest it sells in the 28 August 2026 snapshot.

A cheap tier already existed at the impossible end. Thirteen days before
GPT-4 shipped, OpenAI released gpt-3.5-turbo,
[priced at "$0.002 per 1,000 tokens"](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/01/openai-launches-an-api-for-chatgpt-plus-dedicated-capacity-for-enterprise-customers/)
— $2 per million. A million tokens of commercial text generation cost $2
on 14 March 2023, not $60, if the cheapest model was what you wanted.

Compared consistently the fall is real, and smaller than the headline pair
suggests. Cheapest to cheapest, $2 against $0.40. At the expensive end,
$60 against the $20.00 per million output that the same 28 August 2026
sheet lists for gpt-5.6-sol, the dearest of the flagship models on it.
Both pairings fall, and that direction is the durable claim; the ~150x is
an artifact of reading across tiers.
