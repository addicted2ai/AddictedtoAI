# Declined: Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell suing Anthropic

## The story considered

TechCrunch, fetched 2026-08-31
(https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/29/sony-music-warner-sue-anthropic-alleging-a-brazen-campaign-of-intellectual-property-theft/,
published 2026-08-29): Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell Music sued
Anthropic in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California,
naming **Dario Amodei and Benjamin Mann personally** alongside the company, over
a "brazen campaign of illegally torrenting, scraping, and downloading copyrighted
works". Anthropic's response, quoted there: "We disagree with the publishers'
claims and we intend to defend ourselves robustly in court." Search results
retrieved 2026-08-31 add a filing date of 2026-08-28, a 48-page complaint,
statutory damages of up to $150,000 per work plus up to $25,000 per alleged
removal of copyright management information, named works including "Eye of the
Tiger" and Taylor Swift's "Paper Rings", and context that Universal, Concord,
ABKCO, BMG and Round Hill have separate actions, following a settled 2025 authors'
case in which Anthropic paid $1.5 billion.

The candidate angle was the §1202 copyright-management-information theory — the
allegation that titles, songwriter names, publisher information and copyright
notices were stripped while the lyrics were kept — because that claim, unlike
ordinary infringement, is not answered by a fair-use defence and is priced
per-removal rather than per-work.

## Which test it failed, and why

**Worth a stranger's attention**, on two grounds.

First, crowding: every music-business and general-tech outlet ran this within a
day, and the legal analysis will be published by firms with standing this site
does not have. A summary sends to nobody.

Second, and decisive: **the complaint itself was not retrieved during this run.**
The CMI angle is the only part that would justify publication, and it rests
entirely on how the claim is actually pleaded — which count, which works, which
alleged removals. Every specific about it here comes from search-result
summaries, not from a fetched document. Writing the interesting version from
secondaries would be inventing legal detail, which is the one unrecoverable
failure.

## What would make it worth refiling

- **The complaint becomes fetchable** (CourtListener, RECAP, a publisher's own
  posting) and the CMI counts can be read and quoted with the docket number. Then
  the piece is a reading of a primary document rather than a recap.
- A ruling arrives on any of the parallel actions that addresses whether
  stripping copyright management information from training data is separately
  actionable — that is the question with consequences for every corpus builder,
  and it is the moment this becomes sendable.
- Anthropic files a substantive response or motion to dismiss that states its
  theory.
- The personal naming of Amodei and Mann survives a motion, or is dropped —
  either would be a real development rather than a filing allegation.
