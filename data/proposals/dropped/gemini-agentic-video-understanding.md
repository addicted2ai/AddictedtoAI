This one was weighed deliberately rather than skimmed, because **`video` is a
domain in the closed vocabulary and this site has published nothing in it**.
That is exactly the situation the criteria warn about: a quiet domain invites a

## Which frontier criterion it was weighed against, and why it failed

**F1** — "a capability shown for the first time, with an artifact anyone can
check (executed transcript, paper with code, public demo)." Agentic video
understanding is a plausible F1 shape: it is a capability claim about something
models could not previously do well. It fails on the artifact. This run found no
paper, no released evaluation, and no third-party reproduction — only a product
announcement on the vendor's own blog. A capability "shown for the first time"
on a page written by the party that benefits from the claim, with nothing a
reader can run, is a vendor claim about a feature, and this corpus has a
criterion for vendor claims (F4) that requires them to be about a **new
ability** and labelled unverified — which would at best make this a labelled
marketing claim rather than a frontier record worth showing.

**The domain stays quiet, deliberately.** Nothing qualified in `video` this
sweep, and that is a finding rather than a gap.

## Which of the two tests it failed

**Worth a stranger's attention.** "Gemini can now understand video agentically"
is not sent to a specific person with no further explanation; it is a feature
line. The corpus's editorial rule that breadth lives in the data layer and the
bar applies to prose is the relevant one — if this belongs anywhere it is as a
feed fact, not as a post.

## What would make it worth refiling

- A benchmark result on a published video-understanding index with a lead
  change, which would be F2.
- A public demo or an executed transcript anyone can reproduce, which would
  convert it into a live F1 candidate.
- An independent evaluation contradicting or confirming the claim.
