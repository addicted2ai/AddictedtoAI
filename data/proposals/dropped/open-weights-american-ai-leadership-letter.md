# Declined: the "Open Weights and American AI Leadership" letter

## The story considered

A three-page industry letter titled "Open Weights and American AI
Leadership", posted by NVIDIA's Jensen Huang in what several outlets
describe as his first post on X, arguing against premature restrictions on
open-weight models — that they widen economic access, prevent a few firms
controlling the technology, and improve security by letting more researchers
audit and patch rather than concentrating single points of failure. The
prompt was reportedly a White House proposal to restrict US use of Chinese
models. The angle considered was the signatory list as a map of the policy
fight.

## Which test it failed, and why

**True, checkable and current — it fails on both halves.**

*Currency.* The letter is dated 24 July 2026, six weeks before this sweep.
It surfaced today only because a RedMonk commentary piece
(https://redmonk.com/sogrady/2026/09/03/open-weight-models/, retrieved
2026-09-06) discussed it on 3 September. A commentary about a six-week-old
letter is not an event.

*Checkability, and this is the sharper reason.* The reporting retrieved on
2026-09-06 flatly contradicts itself on the fact the story turns on.
Tom's Hardware and TheNextWeb both frame the story as OpenAI, Anthropic and
Google being **absent** from 25 signatories. The RedMonk piece, read through
WebFetch on the same day, states that those three "were initially absent but
joined by the following day" and that 235 companies had signed by week's
end. Those cannot both be true, and the entire editorial value of the story
is which one is. No copy of the letter itself, with its signature list, was
located during this sweep — every source is a description of it.

## What would make it worth refiling

- The letter's own text and signature list, at a stable URL, so the
  signatory question is settled against the document rather than against
  four descriptions of it. That alone might justify a `verify` job before
  any post.
- A dated development that makes the July letter current again: the White
  House proposal advancing to a rule, an executive order, or a named lab
  changing its position on the record.

`data/proposals/dropped/` is a record, never a block. This slug suppresses
nothing.
