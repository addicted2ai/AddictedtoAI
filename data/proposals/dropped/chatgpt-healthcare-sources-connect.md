# Declined: ChatGPT for Healthcare connecting to Epic EHR

## The story considered

OpenAI's 2026-09-01 product post, "Healthcare organizations can now connect
EHR and additional industry data to ChatGPT" — fetched 2026-09-02,
https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-connects-health-records-and-healthcare-sources/.
The announcement: a new Epic EHR integration bringing authorized patient
context into ChatGPT for Healthcare (two modes: EHR context in ChatGPT, and
ChatGPT inside the EHR layout), a Healthcare Public Data plugin with
connectors to nine official sources (ClinicalTrials.gov, CMS Coverage,
RxNorm, DailyMed, PubMed among them), and OpenAI's own physician-evaluation
numbers — 700,000+ model responses reviewed by hundreds of physicians, 99.1%
of responses rated safe across 4,363 ratings on 27 clinical use cases, and
>93% "good" or better accuracy on five connected data sources. Named launch
partners: AdventHealth, Baylor Scott & White Health, Boston Children's
Hospital, Cedars-Sinai, HCA Healthcare, Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer
Center, and UCSF.

## Which test it failed, and why

**Worth a stranger's attention.** Every number in the piece is OpenAI's own
evaluation of its own product, with no independent party, no regulatory
action, and no measured deployment outcome to check. The sendable form —
"ChatGPT can now read your hospital's records" — is a fact a reader would
nod at, and the interesting questions (what does Epic think, what does a
hospital's compliance officer think, is there a BAA posture change) are not
answered by the announcement. It is a vendor product note with pilot
quotes, correctly dated and sourced but without a stranger-visible hook.

## What would make it worth refiling

- An independent assessment: a hospital or health system reporting a
  measured outcome (adoption, error rates, time saved) with a date.
- A regulatory action or compliance finding involving the integration.
- A named competitor response that turns the integration into a market
  event rather than a product feature.
