## The two required changes

Both are in the opening paragraph, both are checkable from the diff alone, and
both are one-line fixes. Everything else I checked held — see below.

**1. "three years" is sourced by nothing.** The piece opens: *"A row on a public
router is the thing Poolside spent three years arguing against."* Nothing in the
diff carries a founding date or any other anchor for a three-year span, and the
oldest thing the entry cites is the AWS post of 2024-12-04 — twenty-one months
before the accessed date, not three years. I looked for an anchor rather than
assuming there was none: I fetched `poolside.ai/`, the four cited blog posts and
the two linked ones (Redpanda, Dell) and searched all seven for `founded`,
`since 20\d\d`, `three years` and `in 202[0-4]`. The only hit anywhere was in
the Fern Labs post, and it is about **Fern Labs**: "founded by Ash Edwards, Alex
Goddijn, and Taylor Young after leading complex deployments at Palantir". A
grep for `20(2[0-3])` across all nine fetched pages returned hits only inside
SVG path coordinates (`68.3593`, `5.40729`…), not years.

This matters more than its word count because it is the first sentence and it is
the frame the whole piece hangs on. The *shape* of the claim is well evidenced —
the AWS, Redpanda, Fern, Dell and Platform posts really do read as an
enterprise/on-prem record, and the Platform pitch quote really is an argument
for not sending data outside a security boundary. It is only the duration that
is unsupported. Fix: drop the number, or cite a founding date.

**2. "Six days before that Platform post, on 28 April 2026" — it is seven.** The
entry's own timeline dates the Platform post `2026-05-05` and I confirmed that
from the page itself. 28 April → 5 May 2026 is seven days. The entry contradicts
its own front matter, which is the kind of thing a skeptical reader checks first.

## On the two things the brief demanded that are not here

Both absences are correct, and I checked rather than assumed.

- **`publishes_from`** is not implemented. `grep publishes_from` across every
  `.mjs` in the worktree returns nothing — it exists only in the pending
  `separate-a-claim-from-a-fact` delta, and the front-matter schema rejects
  unknown keys, so declaring it would fail the build. It would also be
  redundant here: the only domain the entry cites is `poolside.ai` (and
  `trajectories.poolside.ai`, which reduces to it), whose registrable label
  `poolside` is one of the subject's own name tokens, so the vendor test passes
  without a declaration. No org entry in the corpus carries the field.
- **`feeds`** is absent, which the brief itself allows ("an org with no feed
  binding still renders an all-blank row, which is allowed and honest"). No org
  entry in `content/wiki/org/` declares one — the `openrouter-models` rows are
  models, and the four Poolside rows are bound on the four model entries, where
  the join belongs. An empty binding is honest; a wrong one would not be.

Filing the vendor quotations as `cited` facts rather than claim records is also
correct today: `content/claims/` does not exist, the claim schema is unbuilt,
and this is the same shape the recently merged `org/nous-research` entry uses.

## Why this is `revise` and not `approve`

The entry is the best-sourced thing I have reviewed on this loop — I checked
roughly two dozen distinct claims and every one held, several to the digit, and
the two apparent misses were my own instrument both times. It clears the
editorial bar without difficulty: the XS.2/XS 2.1 licence-flip-at-identical-
parameter-count is a genuine assembly, and reading a vendor's own comparison
table back against its own headline claim is the kind of thing an enthusiast
does not get from a newsletter. If the lede is repaired I expect to approve it.

But the two defects are both in the first paragraph, both are numbers, and one of
them a reader cannot check at all. A piece whose entire argument is *the vendor's
specific numbers hold up and its unbounded phrasing does not* cannot open with an
unsourced duration and a date subtraction that contradicts its own timeline.
Neither is a manufactured objection and neither is a matter of taste; both were
measured, and both are one line to fix.
