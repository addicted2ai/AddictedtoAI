The premise underneath the whole repair — that a date move emits nothing — I
checked directly rather than inferring it from the registry. Every line in
`data/changes.jsonl` whose `row_id` contains `nex-n2`:

```
2026-09-01 field_change status active -> deprecated
2026-09-01 field_change status active -> deprecated
```

Two records, both the 2026-09-01 status flip, nothing on 2026-09-02 when the
date moved four days. The mechanism is in the registry: `openrouter-models`
lists `material_fields` of `price_input`, `price_output`, `context_window` and
`status` only, and carries `expiration_date` solely as `status_rule.path`. So a
date that moves without crossing a status boundary is invisible by construction
— which is precisely what the new clause is hedging against, and it is measured,
not reasoned.

## Spec question I resolved rather than blocked on

`openspec/specs/loop/spec.md:31` says "`data/changes.jsonl` stays append-only:
the annotation is a new line keyed to the change it interprets", and this diff
rewrites a line in place. Read literally that is a `spec-violation`; read as a
constraint on `interpret` (an annotation must not mutate the mechanical row it
comments on) it is clean. Merged practice settles it: `git show --stat dffa630`
is "job j-20260904-27 (repair): clear the 3 carried findings on the GLM 4.7
Flash successor annotation", `data/changes.jsonl | 2 +-` — the identical shape,
approved and merged on 2026-09-04. A repair clearing a carried finding by
editing an annotation's own `text`, leaving `key`/`date`/`job` untouched, is
established practice, and no Pulse-produced diff line is touched here. Not a
violation. The ambiguity itself is already filed as
`data/proposals/settle-what-append-only-means-for-changes-jsonl.md`, so I am not
re-filing it.

## Note on the prose

The added clause is longer than the carried finding's "one word", and the
sentence it lands on is now long. I considered whether that is padding and
concluded it is not: the finding asked for a hedge, and a bare hedge ("if that
date holds") with no evidence behind it would be a weaker annotation than the
one it replaced — a reader would have no way to tell whether the caveat was
boilerplate or observed. Naming the two rows that actually slid makes the hedge
falsifiable, which is the standard the rest of this log is written to.
