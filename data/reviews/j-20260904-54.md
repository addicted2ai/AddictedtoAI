sits under:

## Why this is an approve

The original defect was a causal claim ("the response is not byte-stable")
carrying a measurement that spanned one second when the gap it explained
spanned 19 hours. The replacement does not repeat that: it separates the two
scales explicitly, names which one was measured before, tabulates four dated
runs, and — the part I think earns it — does not stop at the byte counts. It
adds the one check that distinguishes "drift" from "the page changed": both
disputed phrases present twice in the raw bytes of all four fetches, matching
what the 03:27 transcript recorded. ~200 bytes moved while the text did not.
That is a measurement of the thing the sentence claims, not a restatement of
the intent.

It stays inside the outcome: the target file, its transcript, and the deletion
of `data/carried/j-20260904-51-carry-1.md`, which is the retirement the brief
required. `git show --stat b639326` shows three files and nothing else.

Three things I am not blocking on are in `carry:` — the 22:53 run took 5.3
seconds by its own transcript and is called "inside a single second"; the 20:32
row silently merges two different header configurations; and my own fifth run
lands a floor below the 22:53 floor, which is worth a half-clause so the
rising-floor observation is not read as a law. None of them touch the
conclusion, which is now supported at the scale it is asserted at.
