Checklist: wiki entry (event). Sources fetched 2026-08-28.

- aclanthology.org/www.mt-archive.info/90/MTNI-1996-Hutchins.pdf: resolves,
  and is Hutchins, "ALPAC: the (in)famous report", MT News International 14,
  June 1996, pp. 9-12. WebFetch could not read the PDF, so I inflated its
  content streams locally and read the full text. Every quoted string in the
  entry appears verbatim: "There is no emergency in the field of translation.
  The problem is not to meet some nonexistent need through nonexistent
  machine translation."; "we do not have useful machine translation [and]
  there is no immediate or predictable prospect of useful machine
  translation." (the bracketed [and] is Hutchins's own); "the earlier samples
  are more readable than the later ones"; "were as deceptively encouraging as
  'machine translations' of general scientific text have been uniformly
  discouraging."; "the facts are that the supply exceeds demand."; "Machine-
  aided translation may be an important avenue toward better, quicker, and
  cheaper translation"; "What machine-aided translation needs most is good
  engineering."; "to bring to an end the substantial funding of MT research
  in the United States for some twenty years"; "For years afterwards, an
  interest in MT was something to keep quiet about; it was almost shameful."
- Same source, every number checked: April 1964 establishment by the JALPG
  of DoD/NSF/CIA under Pierce of Bell Telephone Laboratories; report pages
  1-18 on translation economics ("The first half of the report (pages 1 to
  18)"); 76% of all articles in 1965; 200 hours; 4000 JPRS translators under
  contract with 300 used on average each month; $9 to $66 per 1000 words;
  FTD 109 days against a 65-day minimum; four contemporary systems compared
  with the twelve sentences of the Georgetown-IBM demo of January 7, 1954;
  Publication 1416, 124pp. All match exactly.
- The $20M re-audit is Hutchins's, not the author's inference: "$35,033 on
  sponsoring three conferences", "$59,000 on ALPAC itself", "$101,250 in
  support of research outside the United States", USAF/Navy/Army global sums
  "totalling $11,906,600" with no recipients listed and much of it "in
  support of developments in computer equipment rather than MT research",
  concluding "may well have been nearer $12-13 million". The entry's
  attribution ("Hutchins's re-audit of Appendix 16") is correct.
- Aftermath details verified against the same text: Wayne State to 1972,
  Texas to 1975, Georgetown "had received no funding after 1963", and the
  1984 panel (Hutchins cites King, "When is the next ALPAC report due?",
  Coling84). The entry's "did not matter" framing of the methodological
  unfairness is Hutchins's own point, not the author's.
- nationalacademies.org/read/9547/chapter/1: resolves; confirms the title
  "Language and Machines: Computers in Translation and Linguistics", 1966,
  NAP, 124 pages. Worth recording for a later pass: Hutchins twice writes
  "Languages and machines" (plural), and the entry follows the National
  Academies form. The entry sourced that one fact to the National Academies
  rather than to Hutchins, which is the correct call — do not "fix" it to
  Hutchins's spelling.
- Not independently verified: nothing material. The report's own PDF was not
  opened; every claim is carried by Hutchins's extensive quotation of it,
  which is what the entry says it is doing.

Clears the bar on all three counts. The payload an enthusiast does not have:
that the report's lethal chapter was an economics chapter, and that its
most-quoted number is soft by roughly 40% — both traceable to a named
appendix. It is specific to the dollar and the day, and it is exactly the
page you would paste at someone who thinks ALPAC was a verdict on whether
machine translation could work. Approve.
