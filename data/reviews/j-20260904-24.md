## The rest of the paragraph, checked because the comment was edited too

The revised HTML comment repeats the pre-existing claim that the batch row's
"own last-known pricing was $0.60 in / $3.60 out per million tokens". I read
every historical blob of both snapshot files out of git (`execFileSync` on
`git show <sha>:<path>` from a Node script, per the Windows note), and it
holds, as does the surrounding narrative:

```
latest.json  2026-09-04  batch= ABSENT              std= 0.000000625/0.000003125
latest.json  2026-09-03  batch= ABSENT              std= 0.0000006/0.0000024
latest.json  2026-09-02  batch= 0.0000006/0.0000036 std= 0.000000625/0.000003125
latest.json  2026-09-01  batch= 0.0000006/0.0000036 std= 0.0000005/0.0000022
latest.json  2026-08-31  batch= 0.0000006/0.0000036 std= 0.0000005/0.0000022
   (0.0000006/0.0000036 and 0.0000005/0.0000022 unchanged back to 2026-08-28)
```

That is $0.60 / $3.60 for the batch row on every snapshot that carried it,
absent from 2026-09-03 onward — matching "left the OpenRouter catalog in the
2026-09-03 snapshot, a day after it was last listed" — and a standard row
running $0.50/$2.20 through 09-01, $0.625/$3.125 on 09-02, $0.60/$2.40 on
09-03, $0.625/$3.125 on 09-04. The dated move and the reversal are the two
ends of that sequence.

The clause does not rot: it is pinned to a named snapshot date, so a further
price move makes it stale in no way that matters — it says what 2026-09-04
listed, not what today lists.
