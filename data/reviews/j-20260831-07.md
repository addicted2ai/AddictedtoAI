## The required change

The final paragraph reads:

> All four accounts below were retrieved on 31 August 2026, and the malware list
> and the three response actions appear in every one.

**Search Engine Journal does not carry the third response action.** Fetched
2026-08-31, asked twice with differently-worded prompts:

> **Search for "Refund" or "Reimburse"** — The words "refund," "refunded," or
> "reimburse" do **not** appear anywhere on this page.

and, on the first pass:

> No mention of refunds. The email states: "Your current plan continues for the
> billing period you've already paid for."

The email as SEJ reproduces it has a "What we've done" section with exactly two
items — sessions signed out, saved payment method removed — and ends at "you'll
need to add a payment method again in Settings." The refund is BleepingComputer's
own reporting ("The company is signing affected users out of Claude, removing
saved payment methods, and refunding charges it identifies as unauthorized"),
and it is also in CyberPress ("refunded confirmed fraudulent transactions") and
Cybersecurity News ("issuing refunds"). Three of four, not four of four.

Fix either half: cut the corroboration boast, or scope it honestly — the malware
list does appear in all four, and the refund appears in three. The same paragraph
carries the related soft spot: the body's refund sentence ("Anthropic also
refunded charges it identified as unauthorized") sits at the end of a paragraph
attributed to Search Engine Journal, so a reader who follows that link to check
it will not find it. Attach it to BleepingComputer, which is already the anchor
and already linked.

This is a one-sentence repair and I would approve the post with it made. Cutting
the closing paragraph entirely fixes the factual defect and the one passage of
machine-made prose in a single edit — see `reads-human`.

## One non-blocking observation, not a required change

"They are commodity credential thieves that arrive ... through ordinary downloads
and malicious apps" softens the source slightly. BC's phrasing is "downloads or
malicious apps"; Anthropic's email says "an unofficial download or a malicious
app", and BC notes the Redditor traced it to a pirated game. "Ordinary" reads as
"any normal download", which is a shade broader than what either source says.
Defensible as written — I am not requiring the change — but "unofficial" is both
more accurate and more useful to a reader trying to work out whether it was them.
