## The worked example: every number confirmed against the two snapshots

The proposal's central factual claim is a table and a paragraph measured from
`data/sources/openrouter-models/previous.json` and `latest.json`. I wrote a
script that re-derives all of it and ran it against the worktree's own files:

```
prev 2026-09-04T06:00:03.738Z row_count 427 rows 427
cur  2026-09-05T06:00:04.599Z row_count 431 rows 431
prev scored 164 | cur scored 52
{ lost: 113, gone: 1, lower: 50, higher: 0, same: 0 }
scored rows new to latest 2
```

(`scored` = a finite numeric `benchmarks.artificial_analysis.intelligence_index`;
`lost` = present in the later snapshot with the score gone; `gone` = absent from
the later snapshot; `lower`/`higher`/`same` compared against the earlier value.)

Against the diff, item by item:

- `fetched_at` `2026-09-04T06:00:03.738Z`, 427 rows — matches.
- `fetched_at` `2026-09-05T06:00:04.599Z`, 431 rows — matches.
- "164 | 52" in the table — matches.
- "113 were still present and had lost the score, 1 had left the snapshot, and
  50 still carried a score — all 50 lower than before, none higher, none
  unchanged" — matches exactly, including the three-way zero.
- "Two rows new to the later snapshot carry a score, which is how 50 carried
  forward becomes 52" — matches, and 50 + 2 = 52 closes.

Worth recording because it is the one place the diff is *more* precise than the
brief that commissioned it: the brief said "114 models lost their score
entirely". The measurement is 113 lost-while-present plus 1 that left the
snapshot. The draft decomposes it correctly rather than repeating the brief.
