## The blocking finding

The diff's thesis — repeated four times, in two timeline rows and two prose
paragraphs — is that Spark **1.3** is the *first* Spark-class flagship listed
with an open-weights flag. The source it cites for that says otherwise about
its own sibling.

Fetched the raw HTML (not WebFetch's extractor — its prose is not evidence in
either direction) with a small node script, stripped tags, and grepped the
licence strings across four llm-releases cards. Observed output:

```
... Muse Spark 1.3 Available 🇺🇸 Meta AI · Open weights Meta's successor to Muse

Specifications License Open weights · Meta license (weights pending) Weights Not
released Architecture unknown ...
```

`https://llm-releases.com/models/muse-spark-1-2` (status 200):

(`Open weights` hits = 0, `weights pending` hits = 0 on the 1.1 card.)

`https://llm-releases.com/models/muse-glimmer`: `License Open weights ·
Apache-2.0 Weights Downloadable` — the only Spark-family card whose weights are
downloadable, which the diff states correctly.

So on the cited catalogue the flag appears on the **1.2** flagship card,
released 5 August 2026, character-for-character identical to 1.3's
("Open weights · Meta license (weights pending)" / "Weights Not released"), and
the 1.2 card additionally carries the sentence "Meta has signaled open weights
are coming." The transition away from `Proprietary` happened between 1.1 and
1.2. The 1.3 card frames its own contributor tier with "**as with prior Muse
Spark releases**" — it is not presenting 1.3 as a break.

I looked for a retroactive-edit defence and did not find one.
`https://llm-releases.com/changelog` (status 200, 73,421 chars stripped) carries
the Sep 2 item in full — "Meta ships Muse Spark 1.3, the successor to Muse Spark
1.2 … Standard API pricing $1.25 / $4.25 per Mtok in/out ($0.15 cached input),
with a lower-cost muse-spark-1.3-contributor data-sharing tier. Served by Meta
via OpenRouter." — and it mentions no licence or weights change whatsoever.
Worse for the thesis, the same changelog's Aug 10 entry reads: "Meta released
Muse Glimmer … under Apache 2.0 — **its first open-weight model after the closed
Muse Spark line**." The catalogue's prose calls the Spark line closed while its
card badges say "Open weights"; the diff read the badge as news and did not
check the neighbouring card or the changelog.

The four places that must change:

- `content/wiki/model/meta-muse-spark-1-3.md:56` — timeline event: "the first
  Spark-class flagship listed with an open-weights flag".
- `content/wiki/model/meta-muse-spark-1-3.md:77` — "That is the first
  open-weights flag on a Spark flagship, against a line whose earlier releases
  shipped closed."
- `content/wiki/org/meta-superintelligence-labs.md:58` — timeline event: "the

- `content/wiki/org/meta-superintelligence-labs.md:79-82` — "The line's listings
  moved first on 2 September 2026. Muse Spark 1.3's card on llm-releases carries
  the first open-weights flag on a Spark flagship". The first sentence is

`overclaiming-summary` as well as `false-or-unsupported-claim`, because the
overclaim *is* the framing: the org entry's new paragraph and both timeline rows
are built on the word "first", and the model entry's opening sentence ("whose

**What would fix it, and it is a better piece than the one submitted.** The
finding the sources actually support is the persistence: Meta's flagship Spark
card has read "Open weights · Meta license (weights pending)" since 1.2 on
5 August 2026, and one full flagship release later the Weights field still reads
"Not released" — while the catalogue's own changelog still describes the Spark
line as closed and the only Spark-family weights that exist remain the distilled
Glimmer 30B. A month of "pending" spanning two releases, with the source
contradicting itself about what "open weights" means here, is dated, falsifiable,
and keeps every other line of this diff intact.

**Second required change, smaller.** The org entry's `flagship_weights` fact was
rewritten from `"closed; Meta says it hopes to open-source future versions of
the model"`, cited to Meta's own April announcement, to the catalogue's badge
string cited to llm-releases. That swaps a primary-source statement of Meta's
position for a third-party listing field — and, per the changelog quote above,
that same third party elsewhere calls the line closed. Either keep the Meta-
sourced value and add the catalogue listing as a separate field (e.g. a
`flagship_weights_listing` fact), or state in the value that it is a catalogue
listing rather than a vendor statement. As written the org entry's most
load-bearing fact now asserts Meta's flagship is open-weight on the authority of
a badge.

**Third, in the contributor entry.** `meta-muse-spark-1-3-contributor.md:62-63`
reads "The card states the condition itself:
{{fact:…#contributor_tier_terms}}" — but that fact's `source_url` is
`https://openrouter.ai/meta/muse-spark-1.3-contributor`, not the llm-releases
card the two preceding sentences are about. The value is correct (OpenRouter's
page contains "Prompts and outputs may be used to improve Meta's products.");
the attribution is not. Name OpenRouter's listing in that sentence.
