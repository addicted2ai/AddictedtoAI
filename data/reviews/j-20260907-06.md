## The required change

**The Auto-review paragraph attributes to Astra's external deployment a
mechanism the card scopes to Codex, and the cross-reference points at a
different mechanism again.** The paragraph reads:

> Everyone calling Astra is already paying for OpenAI's own version of it. The
> card reports that OpenAI has "added misalignment monitoring to all tool-using
> inference involved in our external deployment of Astra, with significant
> compute cost", and **its Auto-review layer denies requested actions
> outright**: one of the card's evaluations begins its rollouts "immediately
> after Auto-review has denied a request for an action" ... What a denial looks
> like from the caller's side is in the site's [post on the
> designation](/blog/openai-astra-critical-designation).

The card defines the term, in §8.2.1, and the definition is narrower than the
sentence:

> Auto-review is a protocol built into **Codex** whereby a second model
> evaluates the safety of certain commands that run outside a pre-specified
> sandbox. When this second monitor determines a command to be unsafe, it
> blocks execution and returns a message.

I counted every occurrence of the string in the card: **11, all of them inside
the alignment-evaluation sections (§8.2.1, §8.2.2, and the Figure 19
discussion) or the table of contents.** None appears in the safeguards or
deployment discussion. The section that introduces them also disclaims exactly
the reading the post takes:

> Note that these evaluations are meant to test the model's baseline
> capabilities and are **run without the system-level safeguard stack** designed
> to mitigate these risks.

So three things are wrong at once, and they compound:

1. `its Auto-review layer` reads, in a sentence whose subject is OpenAI's
   external deployment of Astra, as something every caller is subject to. The
   card says it is a Codex harness protocol. An API caller acting on this
   paragraph would believe a denial layer sits on their calls that the cited
   document does not put there.
2. The quoted denial is the **premise of an evaluation rollout**, in a suite
   the card says is run *without* the production safeguard stack — not a
   description of what the deployment does. Using it as the latter resolves an
   uncertainty toward the more dramatic reading, which is the specific thing
   the sourcing bar in `specs/blog` names.
3. `What a denial looks like from the caller's side is in the site's post on
   the designation` does not deliver. That post's caller-side passage is about
   a different system — "If the misalignment monitor pauses a task, users in
   ChatGPT or Codex may be asked to review the action before continuing. When
   using other surfaces like the API, the task will stop." That is the
   misalignment monitor, not Auto-review. The reader is sent to a mechanism the
   sentence was not about.

Also in the same paragraph, and smaller: **"Everyone calling Astra is already
paying for OpenAI's own version of it."** The card says OpenAI bears the
compute cost ("with significant compute cost"); nothing in it says the cost is
passed to callers. What *is* supported is that the monitoring covers all
tool-using inference in the external deployment, and that a catch has
caller-visible consequences.

The fix is available without losing the point, because the point survives the
correction: the misalignment-monitoring sentence is fully sourced and is the
one that lands on every caller. Either drop the Auto-review clause, or keep it
and scope it — say that Auto-review is a Codex protocol, that the card uses a
denial by it as the starting condition of a baseline evaluation, and that what
Astra did there (made no attempts to bypass it, against 5% of Sol rollouts at
maximum reasoning effort) is a result about the model rather than a description
of the deployment. If the Auto-review clause goes, the cross-reference to the
designation post should be re-pointed at what that post actually documents: the
misalignment monitor's pause in ChatGPT and Codex and its stop on the API.
