## The brief's `feeds` / `publishes_from` demand, which the entry does not meet

The directive says the entry "MUST CARRY OR THE ROW IS BLANK" a `feeds` map and
`publishes_from`. The entry carries neither, and the author filed a proposal
arguing both are impossible today. **I checked that argument rather than taking
it, and both halves hold:**

- `publishes_from`: `entrySchema` in `lib/schema.mjs` is `.strict()` and its key
  list is `id, kind, display_name, aliases, status, maintenance, feeds, facts,
  timeline, mentions, themes`. `publishes_from` is not among them, so an entry
  carrying it fails validation. The field is specified in the in-flight
  `separate-a-claim-from-a-fact` delta but not yet implemented.
- `feeds`: the board's org join is by **alias**, not by `feeds`.
  `matchProviders` in `lib/render/frontier.mjs` builds its name set from
  `org.data.display_name` and `org.data.aliases[].name`, normalises, and matches
  against `row.provider` by containment; `feeds` is never read. `grep -l
  "^feeds:" content/wiki/org/*.md` returns nothing across all 17 org entries.
  And since `norm("MiniMax")` is `minimax` and the catalog's provider value for
  these rows is literally `minimax` (measured above), the alias join matches
  exactly — the row renders filled without a `feeds` map.

So the brief asked for one thing that fails the build and one thing that would
have been an invented, wrong join — which the same directive forbids in its next
sentence. Reporting that in a proposal and shipping the achievable part is the
correct behaviour under "report blocked rather than guessing", not a
`spec-violation`. I am not treating the omission as a defect.
