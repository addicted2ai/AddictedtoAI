## One defect, recorded rather than blocking

`data/proposals/dropped/openai-cursor-model-supply-termination.md` states:
"Engadget explicitly notes that no specific contractual provision was publicly
identified." **The source does not say that.** I asked twice, the second time
narrowly. The article names no clause — confirmed, it refers only to a general
contract ("giving Cursor users as much time as it could based on its contract")
— but it makes no statement that the basis was undisclosed or unclear. The
record has converted a source's *silence* into a source's *assertion*, which is
the over-attribution shape this review exists to catch.

I am not requiring a revision for it, and the reasoning is worth stating so it is
not read as leniency. The sentence sits in a record for a story that was
**declined**; nothing publishes from it; the decline itself is independently
sound on evidence I reproduced myself (the 403); and the two things the checklist
requires of a drop record — which test failed, and what would make it worth
refiling — are both present and substantive. Weighed against a second full
invocation on a job already at 15.15 model-minutes, correcting one clause in a
non-publishing artifact is not worth the spend. It is recorded here so the next
scout does not repeat it: *the article does not identify a clause* is the claim
that was available, and it is the one that should have been written.

Related and smaller: the Sony/Warner record carries a filing date of 2026-08-28
from search summaries while the TechCrunch piece it fetched says the suit was
filed late Friday, 2026-08-29. The record labels the 2026-08-28 date as
unfetched and does not rely on it, so nothing turns on it — but the discrepancy
is real and is itself an argument for the record's own distrust of summaries.
