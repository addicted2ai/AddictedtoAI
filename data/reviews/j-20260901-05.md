## Why this is `revise` and not `approve` with a carry

I considered approving with the finding carried, and I want to be explicit about
why I did not, because the practical impact of the stale files is close to zero:
the loop drops derived state before merge (`78af8d4`, `8170be1`, both titled
"drop derived state before merge — recomputed from the merged tree") and
`data/derived/` is a pure function of state that the next Pulse run rebuilds. The
stale rows will be corrected by machinery whether or not anyone touches them.

The commit message will not be. It merges into history as the durable account of
this repair, and it is the artifact a later auditor reads when they want to know
whether the 2026-09-01 retirements were done properly. Three false numeric claims
in that record cannot be fixed by a carry — a carry becomes a follow-up job, and
by then the message is already in history. A revision pass is the only point at
which it is still cheap to fix. That is the entire reason for the verdict.
