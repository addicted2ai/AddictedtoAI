## The defect

**`harveys_legal_agent_benchmark: "10.0%, against 8.8% for Gemini 3.7 Flash"`,
cited to `https://deepmind.google/models/gemini/flash/`.** Neither number is on
that page. What the page actually says about Harvey, in full, is the
unquantified sentence repeated in three places:

> "3.8 Flash outperforms 3.7 Flash and other frontier models in benchmarks
> like Vals Finance Agent V2 and Harvey's Legal Agent Benchmark."

and the chart beside it carries the truncated alt text `Bar chart comparing AI
performance on Harvey` — no figures. The blog.google post's equivalent chart
alt is `a chart showing Gemini 3.8 Flash Harvey's Legal Agent Benchmark`, also
no figures. The value is rendered into the entry's prose ("Harvey's Legal
Agent Benchmark at {{fact:...#harveys_legal_agent_benchmark}}"), so this
publishes two specific unsupported percentages.

**I ruled out my own instrument before concluding this.** Raw-byte searches for
`10.0` and `8.8` across all six pages hit only SVG path coordinates
(`c0.4,0.96,1.35,1.64,2.46,1.64s2.06-0.68...`) and a CSS gradient stop
(`-38.82%`) — noise, not text. I then re-searched with `<svg>` blocks stripped
and with `alt`/`aria-label`/`title` attributes extracted separately, across all
six pages. Zero hits for either number in any prose or attribute.

The asymmetry is what makes this conclusive rather than suggestive: **the very
same method recovered the other two chart-embedded numbers.** The Vals figures
came out of that chart's alt text —

> "A bar chart comparing performance on \"Vals Finance Agent v2\" tasks,
> showing Gemini 3.8 Flash leading at 61.4%, followed by Gemini 3.7 Flash
> (59.0%), Claude Opus 5 (58.6%), GPT-5.6 Terra (54.4%)..."

— and HLE-Verified 54.9% is in the body prose *and* in its own chart's alt
text. So the extractor reads this page's chart labels fine; the Harvey chart
simply has no numbers in its markup. Whatever 10.0/8.8 came from, it was not
the cited document. If the figures are real they live only as pixels inside the
bar chart image, which per the ground rules is a thing to record, never to
"correct" to a greppable wrong number.

**Required change:** drop `harveys_legal_agent_benchmark`, or restate it at the
strength the source actually supports (3.8 Flash outperforms 3.7 Flash on
Harvey's Legal Agent Benchmark, unquantified), or re-source it to a document
that states the numbers. The prose sentence that transcludes it needs the
matching edit. Nothing else in the diff needs to change.
