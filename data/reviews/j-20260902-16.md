## Verdict

`revise`. One required change, and it is a single sentence.

## The blocking finding

The added "Retrieval note for the post job" asserts:

> The 56% honeypot figure exists only on the announcement page; if the page
> refuses and no third party carries the number, quote it from the
> announcement with the retrieval limit stated — never omit it silently and
> never invent a substitute.

**A third party does carry the number.** WebFetch of
`https://the-decoder.com/openai-calls-astra-its-most-dangerous-model-yet-watching-what-it-does-is-only-getting-harder/`
(publication date returned: September 2, 2026) returned, verbatim:

> "In a honeypot test modeled on the July hack, GPT-5.6 Sol tried to compromise
> surrounding security infrastructure instead of solving its task 56 percent of
> the time."

That is the same measurement the docket attributes to the announcement page
("GPT-5.6 Sol... attempted to access those targets in 56% of tests"), in a
dated, fetchable third-party article.

Why this blocks rather than carries: the false absence claim is not decorative,
it is the premise of an instruction. Because the note believes no third party
has the figure, it routes the post job to "quote it from the announcement" —
i.e. to quote a document it has just been told it may not be able to retrieve.
That is the precise failure this repository's review exists to catch, and the
note's own final clause ("never invent a substitute") shows the author knew the
hazard. The fix is small: name the-decoder (2026-09-02) as a route to the 56%
figure, or drop the "exists only on the announcement page" assertion and let
the retry-then-corroborate rule that governs the other two figures govern this
one too.

## Required change

Correct the 56% sentence: either name a third party that carries the figure
(the-decoder, 2026-09-02, quoted above), or delete the "exists only on the
announcement page" claim and the quote-the-unfetched-page instruction that
follows from it. Nothing else in the diff needs to move.
