Checklist: wiki entry (event). Sources fetched 2026-08-28.

- theweekinchess.com/html/twic131.html: resolves and carries the 1997 match.
  Verified: final score 3.5-2.5 to Deep Blue; Kasparov won game one, Deep
  Blue games two and six, games three to five drawn; performance rating 2852
  for the machine against Kasparov's 2795. The game-six header reads
  "DEEP BLUE - Kasparov, Gary 1-0 19 B17 Caro-Kann" — 19 moves and a
  Caro-Kann, both as the entry states. Verbatim: "strictly speaking game 6
  was effectively resigned by Kasparov on move 7"; "he lost the mental battle
  with himself"; and Kasparov's "I resigned today", "the crucial game was
  game two", "I never recovered after this game", "Deep Blue now must enter
  competitive chess".
- theweekinchess.com/html/twic71.html: resolves. Kasparov 4.0, Deep Blue 2.0,
  with the results row confirming he won the final two games. Verbatim: "I
  won this match 4 to 2, but it was as tough as a world championship match"
  and "I played very very seriously"; and the post-game-four quote in full,
  "I'm tired from these games and if I was playing a normal human match my
  opponent would also be exhausted, but here I have something that is not
  exhausted and it's playing with the same strength."
- theweekinchess.com/html/twic70.html: resolves; issue dated 11/02/96 and
  carries "got off to the ideal start for the sponsors with a Kasparov loss"
  verbatim.
- computerhistory.org/tdih/may/11/: resolves; "eventually lost the match and
  $1.1 million purse to the IBM supercomputer" confirms the purse fact.
- **Defect 1 — a claim attributed to a source that does not make it.** The
  entry says TWIC's "full match report treated that game as the first defeat
  of a reigning world champion by a computer at standard tournament time
  controls." I searched both 1996 issues the entry cites, 70 and 71. Neither
  contains such a claim; TWIC 70 goes no further than the "ideal start"
  line and noting the loss was covered widely on TV and in the press, and
  TWIC 71's match report makes no historical comparison. Either cite a
  source that says it or drop the attribution.
- **Defect 2 — an unsourced schedule.** "six games scheduled for the 10th,
  11th, 13th, 14th, 16th and 17th" does not come from TWIC 70 or 71; neither
  lists the game dates. The dates are correct as history, but no cited source
  in this entry carries them.
- **Recorded so a later pass does not "correct" a right claim to a wrong
  one:** TWIC 70's own text reads "The Deep Blue verses Kasparov match got
  underway on Saturday 10th of **January**." That is TWIC's typo. The issue
  is dated 11/02/96, and Saturday the 10th in 1996 was 10 February (10
  January was a Wednesday). The entry's "10 February" and its `1996-02-10`
  timeline date are correct. Do not change them to January on a substring
  check of the source.
- Not independently verified: the aftermath paragraph (Novgorod, "both
  unedifying yet inevitable", the FIDE president and the IOC) is attributed
  to a TWIC issue three weeks later that the entry does not cite by number;
  I did not locate that issue. The 1996 game-one "comprehensively outplayed"
  framing is in TWIC 70 and supports the entry's reading.

Two fixes, both narrow: remove or re-source the "first defeat ... at standard
tournament time controls" attribution, and give the six game dates a citation
(or drop them). Everything else — every quotation, every number, the 19-move
game six and its move-7 verdict — verified exactly.

The payload is genuine and is the entry's own idea: the 1996 match that
Kasparov won contains the sentence explaining why he lost the 1997 one. That
is worth publishing. It fails now only because it puts a historical judgement
in a named source's mouth that the source does not make. Revise.
