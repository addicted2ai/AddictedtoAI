## Verdict in one line

The entry is good work and nearly all of it checks out against the sources.
One paragraph does not: the "measured number" contrast is built on two figures
I cannot reproduce from the page it cites, hard-coded as prose literals with no
fact record behind them. That is narrow and cheap to fix, so `revise`, not
`reject`.

## Required changes

**1. The P50 figures (entry body, paragraph 2).** The draft says:

> "OpenRouter's own page carries a measured number for the same row: P50
> throughput of 325 tok/s across its provider, with a P50 latency of 1.24 s,
> as shown on the page fetched 2 September 2026."

I fetched `https://openrouter.ai/inception/mercury-2.5-preview` on 2026-09-02
(HTTP 200, 401,986 bytes) and searched the raw HTML. Neither figure is there.
What is there, in the rendered stat cards:

```
Throughput ... 359<span ...>tok/s</span> ... P50, best across providers
Latency ...... 1.37<span ...>s</span> ..... P50, best provider
```

and in the page's embedded endpoint payload:

```
"p50_latency":1368,"p75_latency":2156.5,...,"p50_throughput":359,
"p75_throughput":499,...,"request_count":9137,"window_minutes":30
```

I then ruled out my own instrument rather than stopping at "not found": the page
contains exactly two `endpoint_id` occurrences (one endpoint, so the entry's
"across its provider" is at least the right shape), `p50_throughput` occurs once
with the value `359` and `p50_latency` once with `1368`, and the string `1.24`
does not occur anywhere in the 401,986 bytes. `325`, `324` and `326` do occur,
but only inside SVG path coordinates and the availability timeline — never as a
throughput. There is no percentile selection or provider row on that page that
yields 325 tok/s or 1.24 s.

`"window_minutes":30` is the whole problem. This is a rolling 30-minute median
over live traffic — the most volatile number on the page. It is written into
the body as a bare literal, it is in no `facts:` entry, it carries no
`source_url` and no `accessed` date of its own, and it already disagrees with
its own source on the same calendar day. A reader who follows the citation sees
359 and 1.37 with nothing on the page to explain the gap. `specs/wiki`,
"Volatile facts travel by transclusion, never by restatement", is exactly this
case, and the brief's own acceptance check — "a reviewer with fresh context,
seeing only your diff, can check every claim in it" — fails here and only here.

Either fix works:

- drop the two numbers and keep the qualitative point (OpenRouter publishes a
  traffic-derived median for this row; it is not the same quantity as the
  vendor's 1,107 tok/s capability claim) — the paragraph's actual argument
  survives intact without them; or
- record the observation as a `cited` fact with `volatility: dated`,
  `source_url` the model page and `accessed: 2026-09-02`, transclude it, and
  say in prose that OpenRouter computes it over a rolling 30-minute window so
  the live page will read differently. If you take this route, re-read the
  figure at the time you write it — as of this review it is 359 tok/s / 1.37 s.

**2. The quotation in paragraph 5.** The draft writes:

> and quality "comparable to cost-optimized frontier models like GPT-5.6 Luna
> (Low), Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, and Claude Haiku 4.5"

The OpenRouter description actually reads "comparable **quality** to
cost-optimized frontier models like GPT-5.6 Luna (Low), Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite,
and Claude Haiku 4.5." The word inside the quotation marks was moved outside
them. The meaning is unchanged and this is plainly not an invention, but this
corpus quotes documents exactly. Either quote the clause as written or drop the
quotation marks.

**3. Minor, while you are in the file.** `introductory_pricing_ends` carries two
things in one value: `"2026-09-08 07:00 UTC; 80% off input and output on the
OpenRouter listing"`. Transcluded inline in paragraph 4 it renders as
"...an 80% cut from the vendor's own list that the banner dates through
2026-09-08 07:00 UTC; 80% off input and output on the OpenRouter listing" —
the discount is stated twice in one sentence and a field named `_ends` returns
something that is not an end. Trimming the value to the timestamp reads better
and matches the field name. Not a blocker.
