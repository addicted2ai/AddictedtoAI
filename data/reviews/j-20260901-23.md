## The claim under review, and the check that would be red if it were false

The diff makes exactly one substantive claim: that the figures in the
proposal's evidence line are the card's, pinned to the rows they come from.
The check that falsifies it is reading the card's benchmark table. I read it
twice, from two different URLs, so that a single extraction could not be the
only witness — the brief's own warning is that WebFetch's extractor "both


under the header
`| Benchmark | GLM-5.3 | GLM-5.2 | Kimi K3 | DeepSeek-V4 Pro-0813 | Qwen3.8-Max | Opus 4.8 | Fable 5 (w/ fallback) | GPT-5.6 Sol |`.

## The original suspicion, resolved rather than restated

The carried finding worried that "the '29/39' denominator matches neither 130
nor 247, so the pair may be reading across two different timeout rows." The
table answers it: `29 / 39` is not a fraction at all, and neither is
`105 / 130` — they are (2h, 6h) task counts out of 869, and the `247` the
earlier extraction surfaced is the 6h value in the **Fable 5** column
(`181 / 247`), one row, seven columns to the right. The earlier reading
crossed a column, not a row. The repair's reading is the correct one, and it
is the one that makes every number on that row consistent. This is the
strongest kind of outcome for a job of this type: the ambiguity is gone
because the primary document was read, not because the prose was hedged.
