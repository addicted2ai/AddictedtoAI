Checklist: wiki entry (event). Sources fetched 2026-08-28.

- www-formal.stanford.edu/jmc/history/dartmouth/dartmouth.html: resolves to
  the 31 August 1955 proposal. Verified: the four authors and affiliations
  exactly as the entry gives them (McCarthy/Dartmouth, Minsky/Harvard,
  Rochester/IBM, Shannon/Bell Telephone Laboratories); the opening "a 2
  month, 10 man study of artificial intelligence" during summer 1956 at
  Dartmouth College; the conjecture sentence verbatim — "every aspect of
  learning or any other feature of intelligence can in principle be so
  precisely described that a machine can be made to simulate it"; and "We
  think that a significant advance can be made in one or more of these
  problems if a carefully selected group of scientists work on it together
  for a summer."
- Same source, the budget: $13,500 total, $1,200 per faculty participant not
  supported by his own organization (the $7,200 line), $700 for each of up to
  two graduate students (the $1,400 line), $850 secretarial. All four figures
  match. The seven named aspects — Automatic Computers; How Can a Computer be
  Programmed to Use a Language; Neuron Nets; Theory of the Size of a
  Calculation; Self-Improvement; Abstractions; Randomness and Creativity —
  are the proposal's own headings, in order.
- ojs.aaai.org/aimagazine/.../1911/1809 (Moor, AI@50): WebFetch could not
  read the PDF; I inflated its content streams locally and read the text.
  Verified verbatim: "July 13-15, 2006"; the figure-1 caption naming
  Trenchard More, John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Oliver Selfridge and Ray
  Solomonoff; "The attendees did not come at the same time and most kept to
  their own research agenda"; "it was not really a conference in the usual
  sense"; "There was no agreement on a general theory of the field and in
  particular on a general theory of learning."; "to nail the flag to the
  mast"; More "admitted that he never liked the use of artificial or
  intelligence as terms for the field"; "The field of AI was launched not by
  agreement on methodology or choice of problems or general theory, but by
  the shared vision that computers can be made to perform intelligent
  tasks."; Kurzweil's "Turing test-capable AI within a quarter century - a
  prediction with which many disagreed"; McCarthy's "likely but not assured
  by 2056"; the Simon chess prediction "wrong about the 10 years, but it did
  happen within 40 years"; "more than three dozen" presentations; and "Simon
  Osindero working with Geoffrey Hinton discussed more powerful networks".
  The plaque in Dartmouth Hall is in the Postscript.
- Small reading the entry adds and the source does not state outright: Moor
  writes that McCarthy pointed to Newell, Shaw and Simon's IPL and the Logic
  Theory Machine as the summer's important developments; the entry calls them
  work "brought in by visitors". That is historically accurate and not
  overstated, but it is the entry's gloss rather than Moor's word.
- Not independently verified: the AI Magazine 27(4) p.12 reprint citation
  (article 1904) was not opened, and the closing "ten percentage points" for
  ImageNet 2012 is carried by the linked `event/imagenet-2012` entry rather
  than by a source in this file. Both are low-risk; the ImageNet margin is
  reproduced from the official table in that entry's own review.

The payload is the gap between the founding document's confidence and the
participants' own account of what the summer delivered — a budget request
with a charter conjecture attached, and five men fifty years later saying it
was not really a conference. Specific throughout, sourced to two primary
documents, and exactly the page to cite when someone treats Dartmouth as a
consensus rather than a name. Approve.
