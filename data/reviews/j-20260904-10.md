1. **The product-count wording (carry-1).** The brief correctly warned that
   this is a choice between two on-page phrasings, not a correction. My own
   fetch confirms both, each exactly once in the stripped text:

   > More than 35 enterprise products and partner-operated services through
   > the Daybreak Defense Network, bringing Daybreak cyber models into the
   > tools, services, and workflows enterprise defenders already use.

   > Today, our partners across the Daybreak Defense Network are announcing
   > more than 35 partner products and partner-operated services that bring
   > OpenAI's Daybreak cyber models into the hands of the enterprise.

   The author took "enterprise products". That is attributable to the page and
   is the reading the wiki entry's `defense_network_scale` fact already
   supports, so the post and the entry no longer diverge. The post keeps
   "across the Daybreak Defense Network" where the bullet says "through" — a
   preposition, not a claim, and the body prose uses "across" of the same
   network, so the sentence is defensible on either half. Not worth a finding.

2. **"this week" → a date (carry-3).** The page reads:

   > This week's second gathering of utility companies brought together
   > participants representing 40 states and the District of Columbia that
   > collectively provide essential services to more than half of the U.S.
   > population.

   The page's own dateline is "OpenAI September 3, 2026" (found at index
   34,497 of the stripped text). 3 September 2026 is a Thursday
   (`new Date(2026,8,3).getDay()` → 4), so "the week of 3 September 2026"
   names the week the announcement's "this week" refers to. The post's
   surviving claim — 40 states plus DC, more than half the U.S. population —
   matches verbatim. Note the page uses "this week" a second time
   ("This week, we published OpenAI's approach…"); the post resolves only the
   gathering sentence, which is the one it quotes.
