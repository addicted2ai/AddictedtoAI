Checklist: Impossible-to-Routine delta, end A a contemporary chess newsletter,
end B a vendor-adjacent news report. Sources fetched 2026-08-28.

- https://theweekinchess.com/html/twic131.html: resolves. Issue dated 12th May
  1997; gives the match result 3.5-2.5 to Deep Blue over six games in New York,
  and refers to "Kasparov's loss to Deep Blue in New York last night". The body
  note's reasoning — issue dated the 12th, "last night", therefore game six on
  11 May — is sound and is disclosed rather than presented as the source's own
  date. End A is clean, and it is a genuine first-of-its-kind: the first match
  loss by a reigning world champion to a machine under tournament conditions.
- https://en.chessbase.com/post/breakthrough-performance-by-pocket-fritz-4-in-buenos-aires:
  resolves, dated 8/27/2009, matching the front matter. It supports three of
  the delta's routine-end claims verbatim, in one sentence: "In this category
  seven tournament the program, running on a handheld Pocket PC, scored a
  stunning 9.5/10 points, with an Elo performance of 2938." So 9.5/10, the 2938
  performance, and the handheld are all sourced.
- **DEFECT — two claims in the routine end are not in the cited source.** The
  ChessBase article does not say Pocket Fritz 4 *won* the Mercosur Cup, and it
  does not say the field *included grandmasters*. I asked the page for its body
  text twice, with different prompts, to be sure this was not a summarisation
  artifact; both times the tournament coverage amounted to the sentence quoted
  above plus game analysis and product copy. The only rated player named in it
  is "Rodriguez Vila,A (2522)", given without a title.
- Both claims are nevertheless **true**, and I fetched a source that carries
  them today rather than proposing a correction from memory:
  https://www.hiarcs.com/games/mercosur2009/mercosur09.html states "In August
  2009 it won the 2009 Mescosur Cup in Buenos Aires, Argentina" (the source's
  own misspelling), "scoring an incredible undefeated 9½/10 points (9 wins, one
  draw). This is a performance of 2938 Elo", and lists a field including GM
  Rodriguez Vila (2522), GM Valerga (2507), GM Slipak (2469), IM Real De Azua
  (2453) and IM Trois (2342), with final standings putting the program first
  ahead of Obregon and Rodriguez Vila on 6½. It also names the hardware as "a
  HTC Touch HD (ARM/528Mhz) mobile device".
- Fix: cite that page for the routine end, or add it beside the ChessBase one.
  Nothing in the delta's text needs to change — the claims are right, the
  receipt is wrong.
- One conflict to handle carefully while fixing, not to import blindly:
  hiarcs.com calls the event "Category 8" where ChessBase calls it "category
  seven". The delta states neither, so there is nothing to correct, but a fixer
  should not take the category from one page and cite the other.
- Structural note, which compounds the above rather than standing alone: a
  category 7-8 field averages roughly 2425-2475 Elo, and the 2938 is a
  ten-game performance rating against that field. That is a real grandmaster
  tournament win on a phone, but it is not evidence of play "above the
  strongest human players", which is how the capability line reads. End A
  genuinely cleared the strongest human alive; end B cleared a mid-tier GM
  field. The span is real — the interesting quantity is the hardware, not the
  opposition — and saying so would make the delta more accurate, not less
  impressive.

Worth saving. The payload is the hardware collapse: a purpose-built machine
that beat the world champion in 1997, against a consumer pocket device winning
a titled round-robin twelve years later, both dated. But the routine end
currently asserts two specific things its own link does not contain, and on a
surface whose entire premise is a checkable receipt at each end, that is the
defect that matters most. Revise.
