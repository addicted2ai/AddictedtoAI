to hold for that to be right: that six annotation lines carry a date of
2026-09-03 or earlier (so the day-granular qualifier was wrong), and that only
five existed at the moment of writing (so the number is right).

The five job ids named in the evidence block are exactly lines 90, 92, 144, 145

The ordering claim is the one that decides between "fix the qualifier" and "bump
the number to six", so I checked it against the ledger rather than accepting it.


The proposal's `proposed_by_job:` is `j-20260903-02` (07:12Z); the sixth
annotation's `job` field is `j-20260903-06` (15:37Z), over eight hours later, and
the consumed record in this same file names `j-20260903-06 (interpret)` as the
job this proposal produced. So the sixth annotation did not exist when the
proposal was written and is the work the proposal caused — counting it as prior
evidence for the proposal would be circular. Reading line 164 confirms it is that
work: it annotates the same `llm-releases|...|$arrival` key the proposal is
about, and points at the Thomson post. The author's choice of fix is the correct
one.

One correction to the brief's phrasing that does not change the verdict: the
ledger `ts` for `j-20260903-06` is a completion/record timestamp, not a start —
the row records 25.67 model-minutes, so a consumed stamp six seconds later
(`20260903T153729`) cannot follow a start at `15:37:23`. The ordering the
argument rests on is unaffected and is established by `j-20260903-02` at 07:12Z
against `j-20260903-06` at 15:37Z either way.
