## The PDF, not the extractor

I downloaded `https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02849v1` (HTTP 200, 696,079 bytes),
inflated its 147 FlateDecode streams and read parenthesised text literals only.
The extraction carries the usual artefacts — `fi` renders as `034`, and words
break as "con testan t", "o036cial" — so I searched short distinctive fragments.
This mattered: WebFetch's HTML extractor gave me the decontamination sentence as
"...from both the SFT and RL corpora", which is **not** what the document says.
The PDF reads "We exclude all IOI 2025, ICPC 2025, and LiveCodeBench Pro problems
from the SFT and RL data and deduplicate the training corpus against these
evaluations" — which is exactly what the post quotes. The post's quote is right
and the extractor was wrong.

## The contest rules, and the detail the paper leaves out

Fetched `https://www.ioi2026.uz/contest-rules` (HTTP 200) and searched the
stripped text. All three quotations are present verbatim:

- "Contestants may perform at most 50 submissions for each task."
- "contestants must not attempt to access any machine on the network or the
  Internet, other than to access the contest system for usual purposes"
- "There will be two competition days. On each day contestants will be given
  three tasks to complete in 5 hours."
- "Contestants may submit a solution to each task at most once per minute. This
  restriction does not apply in the last 15 minutes of the contest round."

The post's sharpest original finding is that last exception, and its claim that
the paper omits it. I checked the absence against my own instrument rather than
concluding from one miss: "15 min", "15 min utes", "15-min" and "last 15" all
return nothing from the PDF text, while the same extraction happily returns "50
submissions", "760", "535.4" and "per min ute", so the instrument reads numerals
and that word fine. Every occurrence of "15" in the extraction is a page number
or a PDF structure token. The paper does not mention the exception. The post's
statement holds.
