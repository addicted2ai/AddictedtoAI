order inside each sentence. `while the standard row still lists endpoints` and
`or genuinely unroutable` and `HTTP 200, measured 2026-09-02` survive verbatim;
`resolves`→`settles` and `aliased internally`→`an internal alias` are the edit.
A thesaurus pass over a shared skeleton is not less machine-made than the
duplicate was — it is the same uniform block wearing a hat, and the trailing
clause swap in sentence two is the sort of variation that shows up when a
model is asked to make something look different rather than to say something
differently. The finding named two cures — genuinely different sentence order
and framing, or binding the shared half as a transclusion so the duplication is
structural. Neither happened.

**And the reword lost the one thing the sentence exists for.** The old text
named the instrument: *the router's endpoints API returns the model record*.
The new text says *"Anyone re-running this check should note how the record
reads"* — but nothing anywhere on this page describes a check or a record. The
paragraph before it is about OpenRouter's deprecation notice; there is no
antecedent for "this check" and none for "the record". A reader who wants to
reproduce a measurement the sentence explicitly invites them to reproduce now
has to go to the sibling page to find out what to call. The page it replaced
was more useful than the page it produced, on the one paragraph the job
