`deprecated` and the current one reads `active` — which is exactly the
`old`/`new` pair the change record it annotates already carries. The causal
account is the mechanism, not a guess about Z.ai's intentions, and it is
correct.

"A metadata correction" is the one word doing interpretive work — a removed
sunset date could equally be a cancelled sunset rather than a corrected field,
and the source cannot adjudicate. It sits next to the raw observation
("dropped the 2026-12-31 expiration date"), so a reader can reach their own
reading; it does not rise to an unsupported claim.

The text carries markdown backticks around `active` and `deprecated`, and the
home renderer escapes annotation text rather than parsing it
(`lib/render/home.mjs:54`, `escapeHtml(a.text)`; `lib/feeds.mjs:109` does the
same), so a visitor reads the backticks literally under "What it means" on the
front page. This is the first annotation line in the corpus, so it sets the
house style, and `changes.jsonl` is append-only — a later fix is a history
edit, not an append. I still approve: it is cosmetic, the sentence reads fine,
and none of the seven reasons honestly covers it. Strip the four backticks
before merge if that is cheap; do not spend a revision cycle on it.
