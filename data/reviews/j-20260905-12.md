## Two things I looked for and did not find

**A contradiction with the rest of the post.** Section 4.2 of the paper says
the live competition run departed from the standard procedure in its last
round — "we therefore use our available compute to expand the final-round
candidate pool to 1,000 solutions" — while the 502.0 number belongs to the
general five-round pipeline. The post keeps those apart correctly: this
paragraph is about the general pipeline, and the earlier "a five-round
generation pipeline" line describes the competition system, which the paper
also runs for five rounds. Nothing the diff adds collides with the 521.72
mean / 495.0–545.8 range paragraph, which is IOI 2026, a different problem set.

**A divergence between the landing page and the PDF on the sentence in play.**
There is none. The introduction paragraph carrying "502.0 after five rounds of
GenCorrect" is byte-equivalent in both once whitespace and ligatures are
normalised. (The paper's Abstract and its Introduction summarise the same
result in different words — abstract: "Ultra-CC reaches 502" — but that is one
document's two sections, not two documents disagreeing.)

## On the prose

Four short appositives after a colon — "200 generations a round, narrowed to 10
submissions, graded, fed back in" — is the same clipped register the post
already uses ("The batch endpoint is the half that went."). It adds the fact in
the sentence where a reader needs it instead of appending a methods aside, and
it does not soften the point it was inserted into: the paragraph still ends
"Nothing in this paper has reached that number on any problem set." No
`reads-as-generated` concern.
