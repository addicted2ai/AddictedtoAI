## The two things the brief demanded and the diff does not carry

This is the part of the review that could plausibly have been a `revise`, so I
measured it rather than reasoning from the brief's wording. Both omissions are
correct.

**`publishes_from` is not implementable today.** `grep -rn publishes_from lib/`
returns no matches; the field exists only in the pending amendment
`openspec/changes/separate-a-claim-from-a-fact/specs/wiki/spec.md`. Running the
real schema:

```
as authored (shape) -> VALID
with publishes_from -> INVALID: [{"code":"unrecognized_keys",
  "keys":["publishes_from"],"message":"Unrecognized key: \"publishes_from\""}]
with feeds map      -> VALID
```

Declaring it would have failed the build. Carried forward above rather than held
against the diff.

**`feeds` is not the join the board relies on, and declaring one here would have
been worse than omitting it.** The brief states the board joins on `feeds`.
`lib/render/frontier.mjs` joins on alias names — `matchProviders()` normalises
`display_name` plus every `aliases[].name` and matches a catalog `provider` id
when either string contains the other. Measured, with this entry's exact
identity fields and the real 431-row catalog, and with no `feeds` map at all:

```
boardCoverage([bytedance-seed org]): {"orgsTotal":1,"orgsWithRow":1,
  "orgsWithoutRow":0,"providersTotal":58,"providersMatched":2, ...}
```

Two providers matched, not zero — `bytedance-seed` **and** `bytedance`. The row
is not blank, and the join incidentally covers both prefixes the entry argues
are one team. (58 providers total, which is the figure the brief cites.)

The stronger point is what a `feeds` map would have done. `feedRowIndex` in
`lib/changes.mjs:59` builds `${source}|${rowId}` -> doc with a bare `set()`. All
six `bytedance-seed` rows are already claimed by the six
`content/wiki/model/bytedance-seed-*.md` entries — I read
`bytedance-seed-seed-2-1-turbo.md`, which declares
`openrouter-models: bytedance-seed/seed-2-1-turbo`. Feeding the index two docs
claiming that one row:

```
model first, org second -> row owned by: org/bytedance-seed
org first, model second -> row owned by: model/bytedance-seed-seed-2-1-turbo
index size (both orders): 1 1
```

Silent, order-dependent misattribution in the changed feed. Obeying the brief
here would have introduced it. The proposal above is that collision made into a
build error; the entry itself is right to have no `feeds` map, since it binds no
feed-bound facts.

## Volatile values, aliases, prose

The two genuinely volatile numbers the prose compares — the UI-TARS window
against the newest Seed row's — are transclusions
(`{{fact:model/bytedance-ui-tars-1-5-7b#context_window}}` and
`{{fact:model/bytedance-seed-seed-2-1-turbo#context_window}}`), not literals, and
the build resolved both. The literal numbers that remain (27.5/42.5, 88.0/87.0,
54.7/53.0) are dated readings of two specific third-party documents, owned by no
entry as a fact, and quoting them is what makes the argument checkable; that is
not the hard-coded-volatile-value defect the rule is aimed at.

All seven `mentions` resolve to files that exist. The diff is one file, 155
lines, and touches nothing else — no scope problem.

Aliases are classed sanely: two exclusive forms of the team's own name and
`Seed` as `manual`, which is the right class for a word that common. The
side-effect of the bare name on two class-blind joins is carried above, not held
against the verdict.

On the editorial bar: this is not a facts table restated. Four of its five
paragraphs make a claim the data layer cannot: that the router's two prefixes are
one team and why the namespaces disagree; that the listed UI-TARS checkpoint is
the weak one of three and has been superseded for a year; that a language-model
router structurally sees one of six product lines; and that the only callable
Seed 2.1 endpoint abroad is the sibling its own vendor benchmarks second. The
last paragraph leans closest to its facts table, and it earns its place with the
zero-occurrence measurement.
