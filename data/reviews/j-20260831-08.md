## What I checked that holds

**The carve-out mapping — the whole point of the piece — is correct.** Fetched
`digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/general-purpose-ai-models-ai-act-questions-answers`
(page states last updated 9 September 2025, as the post says):

> "The obligations to draw up and provide documentation to the AI Office,
> national competent authorities, and downstream providers (Article 53(1)(a) and
> (b) AI Act) do not apply if the model is released under a free and open-source
> license…"

> "This exception does not apply to general-purpose AI models with systemic
> risk."

The exemption covers 53(1)(a) and (b) only; 53(1)(c) copyright policy and
53(1)(d) training-content summary are not exempted. The post's paragraph is an
accurate statement of this, including the systemic-risk clause.

**The penalty ceiling holds.** Fetched
`digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/enforcement-ai-act` (page states last
updated 24 August 2026, as the post says):

> "fines of up to €15 million or 3% of total worldwide annual turnover,
> whichever is higher"

and the page confirms that incorrect, incomplete or misleading information in
response to an RFI is separately sanctionable. Both halves of the post's
sentence are supported.

**The Regnier figure holds.** Fetched his LinkedIn post: *"The requests were sent
to more than 30 AI companies, including most advanced AI model providers."* The
post attributes it to him correctly.

**Arithmetic.** 2026-08-02 → 2026-08-29 is 27 days; "within four weeks" is
true.

**The traps in the brief were avoided, and this is the draft's real strength.**
The post does *not* imply the RFIs targeted open-weight releases — the header
"The recipients are not named" and the sentence "The Commission has not named
them, and the requests themselves have not been published" keep the obligation
question and the recipient question apart, which was the named defect to avoid.
It states plainly that an RFI "is an information request, not a finding, a charge
or a fine." It declines to make the incidents causal. The affected party is named
twice and concretely. No Virkkunen quotation is presented as anything other than
what it is.

**The anchor renders, and that sharpens the defect above.** `lib/render/blog.mjs`
emits a `section.post-anchor` headed "Primary evidence" carrying each anchor's
date and URL, so the spec's "visible to the reader" clause is satisfied
mechanically. The consequence is that the unconfirmed 26 August date is not
buried in front matter — it is published to the reader, under a heading that
calls it primary evidence.
