## Verdict, and the two changes required

Everything in this post is checkable and nearly all of it checks out. Two
claims do not, and both are cheap to fix.

**1. "Cache reads are the cost most users actually pay."** (body, opening
sentence of *The price change*). This is the topic sentence carrying the
headline, it is unsourced, and the announcement's own numbers contradict it
for the typical case. The arithmetic is on the page: cache reads were cut to
25% of their old price, and the indexed-cost chart puts a typical workload at
75 against Fable 5's 100. If a 75% cut in one component takes 25% off the
total, that component was one third of the total — not "the cost most users
actually pay". The source says cache reads dominate only on the other
workload, and says it explicitly in the chart caption:

> Highly agentic workload covers context-heavy, tool-heavy work, where cache
> reads make up most of the cost.

That caption is a third of a screen above the "$10 / $50" sentence the post
quotes correctly two lines later, so this is not a sourcing gap — it is a
sentence written from the shape of the story rather than from the figures
beside it. Run the same arithmetic on the agentic column and it lands at ~60%
(0.75 x 0.60 = 0.45), which is exactly the announcement's "up to
approximately 45%". A skeptical reader does this division in ten seconds and
the post's credibility goes with it.

*Required*: cut the sentence, or replace it with the split the source
supports — cache reads are about a third of a typical Fable bill and most of
an agentic one, which is why the same 75% price cut produces 25% and 45%.

**2. "since June, when a nineteen-day suspension of both ended with access
restored for approved partners."** The suspension did not end in June. This
site's own entry, `content/wiki/org/anthropic.md` — the page the sentence
links to — puts it at:

```
  - date: "2026-06-12"
    event: "access to Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5 suspended"
  - date: "2026-07-01"
    event: "access restored; Mythos 5 returned to a set of US organisations
            following US government approval"
```

12 June to 1 July is the nineteen days, and it ends in July. The clause as
written attaches the ending to June, and it also compresses three separate
events (announced 9 June, suspended 12 June, restored 1 July) into one month.
The checklist asks for explicit dates; here a date is both implicit and wrong.

*Required*: name the dates — announced 9 June 2026, suspended 12 June,
restored 1 July — or drop the suspension clause and keep the wiki link.

Neither defect touches the reporting that makes the post worth publishing.
This is a narrow revision, not a rewrite.
