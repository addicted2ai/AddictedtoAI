## What the diff claims, and what I measured

Everything below was fetched live on 2026-09-02 from this worktree, and every
snapshot figure was read out of
`data/sources/openrouter-models/{previous,latest}.json` in the branch under
review. I did not re-run `npm test` or `npm run build` — the loop's results
stand.

### Confirmed

- **The cohort is real and the snapshot facts are as the pages state.**
  `previous.json` (date 2026-09-01, 420 rows) holds
  `anthropic/claude-opus-4.7-fast` (in 0.00003 / out 0.00015),
  `anthropic/claude-opus-4.8-fast` (0.00001 / 0.00005) and
  `anthropic/claude-opus-5-fast` (0.00001 / 0.00005), each `expiration_date:
  null`. `latest.json` (2026-09-02, 421 rows) holds none of them; the standard
  and `:batch` rows for all three models survive unchanged. The only remaining
  `-fast` id in the latest snapshot is `morph/morph-v3-fast`.
- **Both fast-mode doc quotes are exact.** Fetched
  `https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/fast-mode.md` (HTTP
  200, 27,680 bytes). Line 29: "Fast mode is not available on Claude Opus 4.7.
  Requests to `claude-opus-4-7` with `speed: "fast"` return an error; unlike
  Claude Opus 4.6 (see the following note), requests do not fall back to
  standard speed. The model itself remains available at standard speed. To
  continue using fast mode, migrate to Claude Opus 5 or Claude Opus 4.8." The
  4.7 page's quotation and its Opus 4.6 contrast both hold. Lines 21–22 list
  Opus 5 and Opus 4.8 as the supported models; line 2 gives "research preview";
  line 40 gives "Up to 2.5x higher output tokens per second"; the pricing table
  at line 224 gives "$10 USD / MTok | $50 USD / MTok" for Opus 5 / Opus 4.8,
  which is exactly twice the $5/$25 base — so "twice the base price" holds.
- **The Opus 5 launch-post quote is exact.** Fetched
  `https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5` (HTTP 200); the body block
  reads "It's also offered in Fast mode, where it runs around 2.5 times the
  default speed. As with Opus 4.8, Fast mode is available at twice Opus 5's
  base price on the Claude Platform and through usage credits in Claude Code."
- **"Anthropic Fast" is the router's own label, at the stated price.**
  `https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/anthropic/claude-opus-5/endpoints`
  returns an endpoint `{"provider_name":"Anthropic","tag":"anthropic/fast",
  "prompt":"0.00001","completion":"0.00005"}`, and the model page's embedded
  payload gives `"provider_display_name":"Anthropic Fast","provider_slug":
  "anthropic/fast","service_tier":"priority"`. The 4.8 row has the same
  endpoint at the same price. So $10 / $50 and the "Anthropic Fast" name are
  both right, and they do match the vanished rows' last-known facts.
- **The 4.7 negative check holds.** The 4.7 endpoints list has eight endpoints
  (Amazon Bedrock, Google, Claude Platform on AWS, Anthropic, Azure) and no
  `anthropic/fast` tag among them — "no fast endpoint among them" is measured,
  not assumed.

### Not confirmed — this is what the revision has to fix

**1. "The slug no longer resolves" / "A request pointed at the old slug fails
now" is contradicted by OpenRouter's own notice.** This is the sentence a
reader acts on, and the brief names it as the point of the job ("the prose that
tells a reader whether they can still call these models"). What I measured:

- `GET https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/anthropic/claude-opus-4.8-fast/endpoints`
  → **HTTP 200**, `{"data":{"id":"anthropic/claude-opus-4.8-fast","name":
  "Anthropic: Claude Opus 4.8 (Fast)",...}}`. Same for
  `anthropic/claude-opus-5-fast` and `anthropic/claude-opus-4.7-fast`. The web
  pages also return 200 (`<title>Claude Opus 4.8 (Fast) - API Pricing &
  Providers | OpenRouter</title>`).
- The `anthropic/claude-opus-4.8-fast` model page carries, in a warning
  callout: "As of September 1, 2026, this dedicated fast model is deprecated.
  Fast mode is now served by the fast service tier endpoint on Claude Opus 4.8
  — request it with service_tier: "fast" or speed: "fast". **Requests to this
  model keep working and are served by the same fast tier capacity, so no
  action is required**, but new integrations should target the regular model.
  Learn more in our service tier docs:
  https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/service-tiers"
- The `anthropic/claude-opus-5-fast` page carries the identical notice naming
  Claude Opus 5.

So for 4.8 and 5 the deprecated slugs still answer and still serve. The pages
say they fail. That reading came from the row's absence in the models list —
an inference from what the snapshot lacks rather than a measurement of what
the router does, which is the defect class this review exists to catch. Both
pages need the claim removed and replaced with what the router published:
deprecated on 2026-09-01, still working, new integrations should move.

**2. The migration instruction is not the one the source gives.** Both pages
tell the reader to "pin the 'Anthropic Fast' provider". OpenRouter's notice
says to request `service_tier: "fast"` or `speed: "fast"` on the regular model.
The provider-pin route plausibly works, but the vendor's stated method is the
one to write, and the notice's own link
(`https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/service-tiers`) is the citation
for it.

**3. The deprecation notice itself is uncited on all three pages, and it is
the source that settles the brief's question.** The brief asked which of four
things happened — retired, renamed, folded into standard service tiers, or
delisted by this one router. OpenRouter answers it in its own words, dates it
to 2026-09-01, and names the mechanism (a fast *service tier* on the parent
row). The pages reach roughly the right conclusion for 4.8 and 5 without ever
citing the document that states it, and the one they do cite — Anthropic's
fast-mode doc — says nothing about the router's slug at all.

**4. The absence of that notice on the 4.7 page is the cohort's real split, and
it goes unremarked.** `https://openrouter.ai/anthropic/claude-opus-4.7-fast`
carries no deprecation callout: no "As of September", no "deprecated", no
"keep working" (I checked each string; all absent), and the saved HTML contains
zero `lucide-triangle-alert` warning elements against exactly one on each of
the 4.8-fast and 5-fast pages. Its API description also
lacks the "Note: As of September…" the other two carry. That is the evidence
that 4.7 was dropped without a successor while 4.8 and 5 were migrated — the
strongest thing in this cohort and it is not on any of the three pages.

**5. "This one was the short-lived member" is false as written.** The
`created` timestamps, read from the live model records: 4.7-fast **2026-05-12**,
4.8-fast **2026-05-27**, 5-fast **2026-07-24**. Of the three vanished rows the
4.7 one lived longest — about 113 days against 98 and 40. If the sentence means
the Opus 4.7 *generation* was short-lived, the paragraph's subject is "all
three Anthropic fast rows", so that is not what it says. Either fix the
referent or drop the clause.

**6. "The row is not coming back" is a prediction, not a measurement.** The
docs support "fast mode is not available on Claude Opus 4.7" in the present
tense and support the migration advice; they do not support a claim about the
future. Worth softening to what the doc actually establishes. Related, and
unexplained on that page: the row was live and priced at 6× standard
(0.00003 / 0.00015 — OpenRouter's own description calls it "premium 6x
pricing") the day before it vanished, while the doc says fast mode is not
available for that model. A reader will notice that the page's stated cause
predates the row it is explaining; one clause acknowledging the row existed
and was withdrawn would close it.

### On scope

The diff touches only the three named entries plus the loop's own `.job/`
files. It deletes no entry and no `feeds:` binding, and it edits no reserved
path. Scope is clean; the front matter of all three files is untouched, which
is right — the vanished-row last-known-value rendering is the designed
behaviour the brief said not to repair. No review record binds any of these
three entries (I grepped `data/reviews/` for all three slugs: the only hits are
seed records for *other* entries that mention the fast rows in their notes), so
no mismatched-review finding arises from these edits.

### What is genuinely good here

The cohort read is right, the sourcing on Anthropic's side is exact rather than
approximate, the $10/$50 identity between the vanished rows' last-known prices
and the surviving fast endpoint is a real check that a lazier draft would have
skipped, and the 4.7 page correctly refuses to assume the whole tier died. The
revision is not a rewrite: it is the "can I still call it" sentence on two
pages, one citation added to all three, and two overstatements trimmed.
