## Verdict

`revise`, one required change, reason `false-or-unsupported-claim`.

**The required change.** The fourth paragraph of "The clause meets a download
you already made" says, in full:

> It does not say what happens next. The document carries no enforcement
> clause, no audit right, and nothing about what MiniMax does with use outside
> the territory.

Two of those three assertions are contradicted by the licence the post is
quoting. I fetched the same raw file the post cites and read all nine sections.
Section VIII.2, verbatim:

> 2. If you breach any term or condition of this Agreement, we have the right
> to terminate this Agreement. Upon termination, you must immediately cease
> accessing, using, and distributing the MiniMax H3 Works; delete or destroy
> all copies within your possession or control; and notify each downstream
> recipient that your authorization has ended. The obligations in the preceding
> sentence and Sections VI.1, VI.3, VII, and IX survive termination.

And use outside the territory is unambiguously a breach by two independent
routes. Section V.4, which the post itself quotes, is a use restriction. And
Exhibit A, the Acceptable Use Policy — incorporated into the Agreement by
reference by Section V.1 ("must comply with the Acceptable Use Policy for the
MiniMax H3 Works, which is incorporated into this Agreement by reference") —
opens its prohibition list with:

> 1. Use outside the Applicable Territory;

So the licence does say what happens next: MiniMax may terminate, and on
termination the reader must stop, **delete or destroy every copy in their
possession or control**, and **notify each downstream recipient that their
authorization has ended**.

This is not a pedantic catch. It is the single most actionable fact in the
document for the exact reader the post names — the person in the EU, the UK,
South Korea or the US who already pulled the weights — and the post tells them
the document is silent on it. The error also runs in the reassuring direction,
which is the worse direction for a piece whose stated send-test is "sends this
to their counsel". The notify-downstream obligation in particular pairs with
the post's own Section III paragraph about teams that already shipped H3
onward, and the post has the pieces adjacent without connecting them.

"No audit right" is correct and should stay. Fixing this is a paragraph, not a
rewrite: state VIII.2, note that Exhibit A item 1 makes territorial use a
breach, and keep the honest qualifier that nothing in the record says MiniMax
has invoked it against anyone.

## Form, anchor and scope

**Form.** I read this as a **synthesis**, not a news note, and judged it on that
finish line. Nothing in it happened this week: the licence took effect 29 days
before the post's date, and the piece's finding is a shape across a document set
— one licence, its Q&A, two API records, a repository history and a 30-row
census — rather than an event. On that reading the absence of `covers:` or
`anchor:` in front matter is correct rather than a `spec-violation`; a declared
anchor of 2026-08-02 would fail the 7-day build check, and manufacturing a
fresher one would be precisely the laundering the spec forbids. The synthesis
bar is met: the derivation is reproducible from the Sources block, every source
carries its retrieval date, and the evidence is enumerable (17/11/2 of 30, two
MiniMax rows, one historical geographic case) rather than impressionistic. I am
flagging the classification explicitly because it is the one judgment in this
review a reasonable reviewer could take the other way.

**Affected party.** Named and concrete: downloaders in the four excluded
territories, and separately teams that redistributed H3 downstream under
Section III. The "what to do" half is the application form at
platform.minimax.io/h3-license, stated in its own section rather than buried,
which the brief specifically required. The required revision above is what
completes the "what changes for them" half.

**Overclaiming.** The title uses the licence's own word ("Excluded
Territories") and the body proves it in the first section. It does not assert
motive, which is the trap here — the AI Act paragraph is the most disciplined
writing in the piece, and it declines the causal claim in the same breath as it
names the coincidence. No `overclaiming-summary`.

**Scope.** The diff is one post, the derived search-index row and payload
numbers the build regenerated, and one proposal file — within the cap of one,
of type `machinery`, so it does not self-amplify. Nothing reserved is touched.
No `scope-violation`.

**Rejection index.** Empty; nothing to match against. On the fuzzy half: the
existing blog corpus has one adjacent piece,
`eu-ai-office-first-enforcement-rfis`, and this is not a re-tread of it — that
post is about the Commission's supervisory step, this one about a vendor's
