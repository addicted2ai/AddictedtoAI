## The charge, checked first

Outward. Nothing filed here could have been written from this repository. The
three candidates rest on arXiv:2609.04699 (a PubMed-wide study of model
deprecation), arXiv:2609.03582 plus a Google announcement, a Google developer
doc, a TechCrunch report, and an OpenAI essay reached through two third-party

says so in the docket — "This half is inward by design — it is the
counterweight, not the story." That is the correct shape. The story is the
paper; the feed is what this site can put beside it that nobody else can.

## The cap and the flag

Three proposal files added. Two are unflagged (`model-retirement-...`,
`pachocki-...`), under the cap of three. The third,
`weathernext-3-tops-operational-weatherbench`, declares `frontier: true` with
`frontier_reason: F2` — the criterion ID alone, as required — and
`domains: [science-math]`, which is in the closed vocabulary. The flag holds, so
it is exempt from the count and the merge keeps all three. Expiries: 2026-09-14,
2026-09-12, 2026-09-13 — all within 7 days of the 2026-09-07 filing date, and
all three are event-driven, so the 7-day bound is the right one and each is
inside it.

The F2 argument itself is the part I pushed on, because a flag is the thing this
checklist is most worried about. It holds: Operational WeatherBench is published,
it is run by a third party, Google's own developer documentation concedes its
independence in the sentence quoted above, and the displaced leaders are the two
operational physics systems. Crucially the docket does not claim F1 — it names
GraphCast in November 2023 as where the underlying capability was first shown and
points at this corpus's own delta for it — and it does not claim F3. It also
states plainly why the launch coverage everyone else ran does not qualify and
what does. That is the criteria being applied rather than invoked.

Two candidates were weighed against a criterion and deliberately *not* flagged —
model-retirement against F5, Pachocki against F4, each with a stated reason
(a retrospective statistic is not a single dated access event; a claimed *loss*
of a monitoring ability is not a vendor claim of a new ability). Declining to
flag a story you want to be important is the harder half of this discipline and
both did it.

## The drop records

Eight, and I checked the form on each. Every one names which of the two tests it
failed and what would make it worth refiling with a concrete, dated condition —
not "if it becomes important". Every one also names the F-criterion it was
weighed against and why it failed, which is the part the checklist says is
unconditional and the part that is easiest to skip on a busy day:
`deepmind-proactive-cyber-defense` F1/F4, `deepseek-v4-flash-vision-exp-open-weights`
F3, `gemini-agentic-video-understanding` F1, `harvestbench-pricing-agent-side-effects`
F1/F2, `iris-open-search-agents-397b` F1, `microsoft-vibevoice-asr-streaming-7b`
F1/F3, `openai-research-acceleration-view-inside` F4,
`vllm-0-28-deepseek-v4-sparse-mla` F1.

Two of them are the ones that make the sweep auditable rather than decorative.
`gemini-agentic-video-understanding` and `microsoft-vibevoice-asr-streaming-7b`
each say in terms that `video` and `audio` are vocabulary domains this site has
published nothing in, that the domain was swept, that the tempting candidate was
weighed, and that nothing qualified — and then decline anyway. The audio record
even lists the adjacent items seen and passed over. That is precisely the
scenario the spec writes down ("A quiet domain is not filled to look busy") and
it is the one place a scout can cheat invisibly.

The `deepseek-v4-flash-vision-exp-open-weights` decline is the sharpest
judgment in the diff: the F3 second limb turns on matching a covered lab's
frontier on a published measure, and the record refuses to round 83.9 on
Terminal Bench 2.1 up to Claude Opus 4.8's 85.0 — "'Nearly matching' is not a
criterion". Declining the hub's number-one trending model on a two-tenths
distinction is the bar working.

I take the standing caveat seriously: eight drop records prove the form of the
bar, not its rate. Nothing here measures how many stories were considered, and I
am not reading eight records as evidence of a wide sweep.
