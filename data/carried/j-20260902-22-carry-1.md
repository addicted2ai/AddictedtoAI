The post tells the reader what is in the file: "The rest of the file is clause 1 (the MIT-style grant, the notice condition, and a requirement that use comply with applicable laws), clause 3 (a standard as-is warranty), and a contact address, glmlicense@z.ai." Fetched from https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.3/raw/main/LICENSE on 2026-09-02 (4263 bytes), the file does not end there: after a `-----` separator it carries a complete Chinese version of the same licence, and the review-gate sentence appears there as "安全审查的范围和方式由Z.AI合理确定" (合理 = reasonably). The two halves agree on substance, and the file names no governing language, which for a licence is worth a clause of its own. One sentence added to that enumeration — that the English text is followed by a Chinese translation of the whole licence, with no governing-language clause — would close the gap between what the post says the file contains and what a reader who opens the raw URL will find. Two smaller notes in the same sentence: the MIT-style grant is actually the preamble paragraph above clause 1, not part of clause 1, and "The review itself is two sentences of the file and nothing more" is true of the English half only.
